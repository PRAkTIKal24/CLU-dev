\paragraph{Reading the CMAZE-T panel figures.} Figs.~\ref{fig:cmazet-families}--\ref{fig:cmazet-door-tint}
(App.~\ref{app:mazewm}) draw single held-out transitions with every model's next state. Each panel
is a $5\times5$ crop of the maze centred on the agent's cell at $s_t$. Walls are dark, tinted cells
pale in their colour, doors framed in their colour with a bar across while closed, untaken keys are
diamonds, the goal a cross, and the agent a black disc whose centre shows the colour of the key in its
pocket (white when the pocket is empty). The first column is $s_t$ with the action (an arrow, or a
ring for pick-up), the second the engine's $s_{t+1}$, and each further column the $s_{t+1}$ that a
model's emitted outcome produces. The engine's judge does not score the picture: it scores four
emitted facts, legality, the move, the key event and the pocket after the move, and a picture need
not show all four. So a strip under every $s_{t+1}$ prints the emitted facts (under the engine's
column, the true ones). Its first line reads legal or illegal. Its second line shows the move
($\uparrow$ $\rightarrow$ $\downarrow$ $\leftarrow$, or a dot for staying), the key event ($+$ and a
colour for a key picked up, $-$ and a colour for a key used at a door, blank for none) and the pocket
after the move (the disc; white is empty). An illegal emission leaves the agent in place and is scored
on its pocket alone, so only the pocket is shown. The tick or cross on a panel is the judge's verdict
on all four facts, so two panels that show the same picture and the same strip never carry opposite
marks.
% SRCBLOCK: w21-maze-t-seed4/FINDINGS.md
\paragraph{Why a hold-out draw can remove a whole colour relation.} The hold-out removes word
combinations from training at random. On some draws, for some colour, it removes every training
transition in which the pocket and the target tint share that colour, the combination that decides
whether a move is legal. A model that codes colour by identity then has no training evidence for the
held-out transitions of that colour and cannot answer them, however it is trained. A model that is
also given the relation ``pocket colour equals tint colour'' can: the relation is learned from the
other colours, where it is seen, and applies unchanged to the colour whose rows were removed. This is
what the relational columns add (Fig.~\ref{fig:maze-wm}(c)), and it is why every seed is reported
separately.
