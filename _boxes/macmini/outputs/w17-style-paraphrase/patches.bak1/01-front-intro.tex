A world model trusted with requests it has never seen must satisfy three requirements
simultaneously: its outputs are \emph{valid} under an exact judge, it \emph{composes}
held-out combinations of factors from factors it has seen, and it can \emph{forget} specific
training rows exactly. Compositional generation is an extensively studied field and exact
unlearning an established one; their intersection, however, is empty: a mechanical
screen of approximately 4,050 works finds no generative model that deletes training rows
exactly. % SRC: _thinkpad/w8/w8-novelty/NOVELTY.md
We therefore study the simplest object with all three properties, the \emph{Naive CLU}: a
label-free store of per-well principal subspaces, computed on the latents of a frozen
encoder, read through a request-indexed address book and rendered by a frozen decoder; its
content is derived from a retained sufficient statistic. Because deletion downdates the
statistic rather than the truncated factors, the store after deletion equals the store fitted
without the deleted rows.
On a Shapes3D value hold-out, with the partition selected on validation ($\Kstar=32$ wells),
the store composes held-out pairs at 32.350~$\pm$~0.815 and held-out six-factor corners at
67.177~$\pm$~1.320 distinct valid novel tuples per corner over three seeds. It beats every
read-path-matched learned rival on 109 of 109 corners on every seed, and all four landscape
controls collapse to exactly 0.0000. % SRC: w12b-nr6-and-kstar/NR6-T7.md
Deletion is exact and store-scoped, but it is not free: across the three seeds, the object
that forgets is 4.0768 to 4.0897 times the 524,288-byte reference in its four-part ledger. We
further show that, on this object, deletion changes what the store holds but not what it answers.

The generation of held-out factor combinations is a long-standing and active problem, which
two lines of work address. The multilinear line (Tenenbaum and Freeman, 2000; TensorFaces,
Vasilescu and Terzopoulos, 2002; Vlasic et al., 2005; MLC-VAE, 2020) factorises a data tensor
indexed by every label combination and renders linearly. The frozen-generator line (GANSpace,
2020; TCA, 2021; PandA, 2023, and its 2024 journal version) identifies editable directions in a
pretrained generator, without labels and without an address book. We claim neither half.
Specifically, we do not claim multilinear or bilinear factored generation, a request-indexed
tensor head, tensor-product label conditioning, or the downdate primitive; the latter follows
in one line from the summation form of Cao and Yang (2015), since a covariance is a summation.

Exact unlearning is likewise established: for $k$-means (Ginart et al., 2019), for bilinear
matrix completion, and for ridge heads on frozen backbones, where the most recent work
explicitly restricts itself to ridge regression and excludes principal-component,
eigendecomposition and generative models. The closest generative attempts are approximate.
Feature unlearning for pretrained GANs and VAEs unlearns a feature rather than rows and
fine-tunes the generator; data redaction from pretrained GANs is stated to be a task different
from deletion; and GAN unlearning by substitution is approximate by construction.

However, on a systematic screen the intersection of the two literatures is empty. A
forward-citation crawl over every title and abstract citing the canonical works of either side
screened approximately 4,050 unique works: 3,038 citing the generation-side anchors, 759 citing
the deletion-side anchors, 254 from 21 orthogonal queries that cite neither, and about 100 from
a final targeted sweep. % SRC: _thinkpad/w8/w8-novelty/NOVELTY.md
Of the 3,038 generation-side works, deletion language appears in 10 (0.3\%), seven of them
false positives and none exact; no work among the 3,038 combines frozen-decoder, tensor-head
and label-index language (0 of 3,038); no single work reaches more than 4 of the 6 claim atoms;
and none of the 759 deletion-side works combines principal-subspace, generative and
frozen-model language. % SRC: _thinkpad/w8/w8-novelty/NOVELTY.md
The orthogonal query that poses the question directly, \emph{compositional} $\wedge$
\emph{generative} $\wedge$ \emph{exact deletion}, returns zero works.
% SRC: _thinkpad/w8/w8-novelty/NOVELTY.md
We therefore state the position only in the form that the screen supports: \emph{the
intersection of compositional generation with exact deletion is empty} in the works we could
screen. The residual risk is real, and \S\ref{sec:limits} states it: abstract-level screening
cannot see a match buried in a method section, and the indexing of preprints younger than about
three months is incomplete.

One distinction within this intersection is ours and merits a sentence. The decremental-PCA
line (Hall, Marshall and Martin, 2002, and successors) subtracts \emph{deflated} eigenspaces;
its authors describe the result as an approximation to the refit, report that the subtraction
tends to instability, and give no rigorous error analysis. Our route is exact for the opposite
reason: \emph{downdate the statistic, not the truncated factors}. We downdate the full,
untruncated second-moment statistic by subtraction and re-eigendecompose the content
afterwards, so truncation error never enters the subtraction.

A world model is useful to the extent that it can be queried for states and transitions it has
not seen, and its answers trusted. We name three properties and use them throughout:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Rule validity}: an output is valid if an \emph{exact} judge, integer arithmetic
  with no tolerance, accepts it; approximate similarity to a training item does not count.
\item \textbf{Composition}: the model produces valid outputs for held-out combinations of
  factor values that it has seen only separately.
\item \textbf{Exact deletion}: after a set of training rows is deleted, the deletable object
  equals, to floating-point roundoff, the object fitted without those rows.
\end{itemize}
The three properties, however, pull against each other: a lookup table deletes exactly but
composes nothing it has not stored; a learned generator composes but can delete only by
retraining; and approximate unlearning misses the exact answer in either direction
(\S\ref{sec:del-comparators}). The object we study therefore makes the three properties
properties of one store, and we measure, row by row, where each holds and where it does not.

We summarise the contributions below, each at the thickness of its evidence.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Composition at a stated partition, against read-path-matched rivals.} On a
  Shapes3D value hold-out at $\Kstar=32$ wells and three scored seeds, the store beats every % SRC: w12b-nr6-and-kstar/NR6-T7.md
  learned rival matched on read-path parameters, on both composition rows and on 109 of 109 % SRC: w12b-nr6-and-kstar/NR6-T7.md
  corners per seed, while every charter control collapses
  (\S\ref{sec:shapes-t7}). The same holds on every rung of an $n$-way ladder
  (\S\ref{sec:ladder}), read with the inference note that, for $n\ge3$, the rungs form a
  training-data axis and not a depth axis.
\item \emph{Exact, store-scoped deletion, with its cost and its bound printed.} For the store,
  value, conjunction and item deletion are light and data-free, whereas each costs the rivals a
  full retrain. The four-part byte ledger is printed beside every row, and a probe exposes the
  limit of the claim: at $\Kstar$ the store forgets the deleted value but the object still
  answers it, because the value identity resides in the address book (\S\ref{sec:shapes-del}).
  Refusal after deletion is a separate, structural mechanism: a hole in a coupling table
  (\S\ref{sec:hole}).
\item \emph{The same properties on a maze, where only deletion transfers through the
  store.} Deletion transfers exactly. Composition on routes is recovered by a plan-space verb,
  which we report as an \emph{interface} result because it lifts a matched-bytes lookup
  floor more than it lifts the store (\S\ref{sec:maze}).
\item \emph{Two ablations and a leaving note.} A composition-only store composes but cannot
  forget, while an item store forgets but cannot compose (\S\ref{sec:ablations}). The
  limitations of the naive unit, its measured zero-dynamics identity and the theory we carry
  close the paper (\S\ref{sec:limits}--\S\ref{sec:followup}).
\end{enumerate}
