ABSTRACT
A world model that is to be trusted with requests it has never seen needs three
things at once: its outputs must bevalidunder the task’s judge, it mustcompose
held-out combinations of factors from factors it has seen, and it must be able to
forgetspecific training rows. Compositional generation is a crowded area and ma-
chine unlearning is an established one. We work at the level of a singlenaive unit
and compare it only with other naive units (an MLP, an LSTM, a transformer and
a continuous-input MLP): we do not claim the best compositional-generation or
unlearning system, and systems built on top of units, such as sharded retraining or
compositional diffusion, are out of scope. We study the simplest object that has
all three properties, thenaive-CLU(n-CLU): a label-free set of per-well principal
subspaces computed on the latents of a frozen encoder, read by a request-indexed
address book and rendered by a frozen decoder, whose content is derived from
a retained sufficient statistic. Deleting rows downdates the statistic, not the trun-
cated factors, so the n-CLU after deletion equals the n-CLU fitted without those
rows. On a Shapes3D value hold-out atK ⋆ = 32wells, the finest partition on
a validation plateau (K= 5...32) that three seeds do not resolve, the n-CLU
composes held-out pairs at 32.350±0.815 and held-out six-factor combinations
at 67.177±1.320 distinct valid novel tuples per held-out combination over three
seeds, with all four landscape controls collapsing to exactly 0.0000. Against four
read-path-matched baselines fitted on the n-CLU’s own 131,072 rows, each also
trained for ten times its selected optimiser steps, the n-CLU leads the strongest,
an MLP, by +7.03 distinct valid novel tuples per held-out combination on its 109
held-out combinations (+7.49 / +8.60 / +5.02 over three seeds, every per-seed
95% interval above +3.73), by +6.54 against that baseline’s best fit of any length,
and by +10.468 to +21.936 against the transformer; on every rung of ann-way
ladder its mean margin over the strongest baseline fitted on the rung’s own rows
is +5.133 to +12.550. After a value, a conjunction or a random 1% of items is
deleted, the n-CLU matches an n-CLU that was never fitted on the deleted rows,
and its address book likewise (content mode deviation at most5.38×10−07, book
statistic deviation 0); the frozen encoder and decoder are outside the object. Dele-
tion has limits, which we print: the book’s retained statistic is 0.97 GB and its
refit takes 2,367 to 5,913 s; the deletable object is 4.0768 to 4.0897 times the
524,288-byte reference in its footprint breakdown; and deleting the conjunction
red sphere changes what the book knows about the shared value: survivors fall
by 6.13 / 6.43 / 6.30 tuples per combination, against at most 0.11 for a random
1% deletion.[TODO:CMAZE-T sentence reserved for the Head: the six-seed
symmetric-input condition table (§4, Table 20) replaces the earlier asymmetric-
input sentence; no number is written here.]We close with what the naive unit
cannot do and with the one measured clue toward a dynamical successor: the n-
CLU is exactly the zero-dynamics limit of a settling unit, with the zero-step arm
bit-identical to the static read.

1 INTRODUCTION:A NAIVE UNIT,AND WHY ITS THREE PROPERTIES MATTER
1.1 POSITIONING:A NAIVE UNIT,COMPARED WITH NAIVE UNITS
The naive-CLU (n-CLU) is anaive AI unit: one model with no system built around it, in the sense
in which an MLP, an LSTM or a transformer is a unit. We compare it only with other naive units, an
MLP, an LSTM, a transformer and a continuous-input MLP, matched on the read path (Sec. 2). We
do not claim the best compositional-generation system or the best unlearning system. Systems built
on top of units, such as sharded retraining, compositional diffusion and physics-informed variants
of an MLP, are out of scope by definition; each is cited in one sentence below, with the unit/system
distinction stated.
Generating held-out combinations of factors is an old and active problem (Du et al., 2020; Liu et al.,
2022). The multilinear line (Tenenbaum and Freeman, 2000; TensorFaces, Vasilescu and Terzopou-
los, 2002; Vlasic et al., 2005; MLC-V AE, 2020 (Georgopoulos et al., 2020)) factorises a data tensor
organised by every label combination and renders linearly; the frozen-generator line (GANSpace,
2020 (H¨ark¨onen et al., 2020); TCA, 2021 (Oldfield et al., 2021); PandA, 2023 (Oldfield et al., 2023),
and its 2024 journal version (Oldfield et al., 2024)) finds editable directions in a pretrained generator
without labels and without an address book. We do not claim either half. In particular we do not
claim multilinear or bilinear factored generation, a request-indexed tensor head, tensor-product label
conditioning, or the downdate primitive, which follows in one line from the summation form of Cao
and Yang (2015) because a covariance is a summation.
Unlearning that matches a retrain without the deleted rows is also established, fork-means (Ginart
et al., 2019), for bilinear matrix completion (Xu et al., 2023), and for ridge heads on frozen back-
bones (Quan et al., 2026), where the most recent work restricts itself explicitly to ridge regression
and excludes principal-component, eigendecomposition and generative models. The nearest gen-
erative attempts are approximate (Kong & Alfeld, 2022): feature unlearning for pretrained GANs
and V AEs (Moon et al., 2024) unlearns a feature rather than rows and fine-tunes the generator; data
redaction from pretrained GANs (Kong & Chaudhuri, 2023) states that redaction is a different task
from deletion; GAN unlearning by substitution (Sun et al., 2025) is approximate by construction.
A systematic screen of both literatures records what we could find. A forward-citation crawl (Priem
et al., 2022) over every title and abstract citing the canonical works of either side screened approx-
imately 4,050 unique works: 3,038 citing the generation-side anchors, 759 citing the deletion-side
anchors, 254 from 21 orthogonal queries that cite neither, and about 100 from a final targeted sweep.
Deletion language appears in 10 of the 3,038 generation-side works (0.3%), seven of them false pos-
itives and none deleting training rows; no work among the 3,038 combines frozen-decoder, tensor-
head and label-index language (0 of 3,038); the highest number of the six claim atoms reached by any
single work is 4 of 6; and of the 759 deletion-side works none combines principal-subspace, genera-
tive and frozen-model language. The orthogonal query that asks the question directly,compositional
∧generative∧a deletion phrase, returns zero works on the screen. These are the screen’s counts,
not a claim that no such model exists: abstract-level screening cannot see a match buried in a method
section, and indexing of preprints younger than about three months is incomplete (Sec. 5). Three
families of systems built on top of units are out of scope, one sentence each. SISA training (Bour-
toule et al., 2021) is a system that makes any unit, a generator included, unlearnable in this sense by
splitting its data into shards and retraining the unit on the shard that held the deleted rows, which
reads that shard’s retained data; we compare with the unit it wraps, whose only route to that match
is the retrain of Sec. 3.4. Compartmentalized diffusion models (Golatkar, Achille, Swaminathan
and Soatto) are a system that trains separate diffusion models on separate data sources, composes
them at inference and forgets a source by dropping its model, so what is composed is models of
data sources, not held-out combinations of factor values within one unit. Physics-informed variants
of an MLP, such as physics-informed neural networks (Raissi, Perdikaris and Karniadakis, 2019)
and Hamiltonian neural networks (Greydanus, Dzamba and Yosinski, 2019), are systems that add a
physical loss or structure to an MLP unit, and the baseline we compare with is the plain MLP unit.
One distinction is ours and worth a sentence. The decremental-PCA line (Hall, Marshall and Mar-
tin, 2002, and successors (Jin et al., 2014; Lee, 2021)) subtractsdeflatedeigenspaces; its authors
describe the result as approximating the refit, report that subtraction tends to instability, and give no
rigorous error analysis. Our route matches the refit, for the opposite reason:downdate the statistic,

1.2 WHY THE THREE PROPERTIES MATTER:WORLD MODELS NEED ALL THREE AT ONCE
A world model (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2025) is useful to the extent that it can
be asked for states and transitions it has not seen (Valevski et al., 2025), and trusted with the an-
swer. We name three properties and use them throughout.Rule validity: an output is valid if the
task’s judge accepts it (on the maze, the engine’s judge, which uses integer arithmetic with no tol-
erance) (Vafa et al., 2024); approximate similarity to a training item does not count.Composition:
the model produces valid outputs for held-out combinations of factor values that it has seen only
separately (Zhao et al., 2022).Deletion: after a set of training rows is deleted, the deletable object
matches, to floating-point roundoff, an object that was never fitted on those rows. The three pull
against each other: a lookup table deletes in this sense but composes nothing it has not written; a
learned generator composes but meets this definition only by retraining (the whole model, or a shard
of it (Bourtoule et al., 2021)); and approximate unlearning (Guo et al., 2020; Golatkar et al., 2020)
is approximate by construction and does not meet it (Sec. 3.4). The object we study makes the three
properties of one n-CLU, and we measure, row by row, where each holds and where it does not.
1.3 WHAT WE SHOW,AND AT WHAT THICKNESS
1.Composition at a stated partition, against data-matched, read-path-matched baselines.On
a Shapes3D (Burgess & Kim, 2018; Kim & Mnih, 2018) value hold-out atK ⋆ = 32wells, three
scored seeds, with every pre-declared landscape control collapsing, the n-CLU scores above ev-
ery read-path-matched baseline fitted on its own 131,072 rows, with each baseline’s convergence
printed beside it: by +7.03 tuples per held-out factor combination over the strongest (+6.54
against that baseline’s best fit of any length) and by +10.468 to +21.936 over the transformer
(Sec. 3.1). On every rung of ann-way ladder it scores above the strongest baseline fitted on the
rung’s own rows, by +5.133 to +12.550 on the seed mean (Sec. 3.2); forn≥3the rungs are a
training-data axis and not a depth axis.
2.Deletion on the n-CLU and on its address book, with its limitations printed.After a value,
conjunction or item deletion, the n-CLU matches an n-CLU that was never fitted on the deleted
rows, and the refit address book matches a book never fitted on them; the match is on the n-CLU
and its address book, and the frozen encoder and decoder are outside the object. We make no
general claim about composition after deletion: deleting a conjunction measurably changes what
the book knows about the value it shares with the survivors (Sec. 3.3). The deletion reads no
training row, but the book’s retained statistic is 0.97 GB, the size of its fit data, and its refit takes
2,367 to 5,913 s, so this is neither a size nor a speed claim. Refusal after deletion is a separate,
structural mechanism, a hole in a coupling table (Sec. 3.5).
3.The same properties on a maze.On a colour-maze transition instrument (CMAZE-T) with
symmetric inputs, six seeds, the n-CLU is ahead of the best matched baseline on three seeds,
level with it on two and behind on one (mean +0.046). A colour-relational request coding lifts
the n-CLU on every seed, but baselines handed the same columns close most of that gap, so it is
an input-representation result, not an n-CLU result (Sec. 4). We offer the transition instrument
itself as a minor contribution.
4.Two ablations and a leaving note.A composition-only n-CLU composes and cannot forget; an
item memory forgets and cannot compose (Sec. 3.6). The naive unit’s limitations, its measured
zero-dynamics identity and the theory we carry close the paper (Secs. 5–6).
Thickness, stated in the introduction.The positive composition claim rests on one environment
(Shapes3D) at three scored seeds; no Shapes3D row can exceed three seeds, because seeds 3 and
4 have verified caches but no frozen decoder and no external classifier, so no combination of theirs
can be judged. The maze transition instrument, under the engine’s judge, prints six seeds on its
words-plus-observation rows (Sec. 4). The rows still pending a run are marked placeholders whose
text names the row.

2 THE OBJECT,THE JUDGE AND THE MEMORY FOOTPRINT
The n-CLU.A frozen encoder maps each training item to a latent. The latent space is cut, without
labels, intoKwells by a balanced correlation partition; in each well the n-CLU keeps the leading
principal subspace of the fit pool, with the pool mean as the first atom. No label enters the content.
We writenfor the number of factors jointly composed by a request andKfor the number of wells,
and never write both ask.
The read.A request is a tuple of factor values. A tensor-product address book, fitted on the fit
pool, maps the request to per-well coefficients; the read rendersx k =U kck in each well’s orthonor-
mal basis and the frozen decoder renders the image. The read is request-only: no feature tensor
enters it, which is asserted on the deployed object (bitwise stable under feature perturbation over 64
checks atK ⋆). Labels therefore touch the model only at the address book. This is the interface split:
unsupervised content, a request-indexed head, and a frozen renderer, with no Cartesian-product
training requirement (Wiedemer et al., 2023; Lachapelle et al., 2023; Montero et al., 2022).

rows downdates it, and the book is refit from it. Neither step reads a training row, but the book’s
statistic is as large as its fit data (Sec. 3.3). We use one definition of deletion throughout, and state
its scope before any table:

and book equal, to float roundoff, those fitted from the retained rows alone. Neither frozen

The frozen encoder and decoder are declared outside the deletable object: they are frozen, so no
deletion touches their weights, which may have been fitted with the deleted rows present. The
scoping sentence we carry is the one our deletion theory states for its own n-CLU: this is an n-
CLU-level guarantee only; the frozen encoder and any residue of past writes in a learned landscape
are separate channels, measured separately; we do not claim certified(ε,δ)unlearning (Guo et al.,
2020).
The memory footprint, never netted.Every n-CLU row prints four byte counts separately: (1)
the atoms, (2) the covariance and count statistic, (3) the address book, and (4) the coupling and
hole table used for refusal. We never net them into one number, and every byte figure names its
convention (symmetric-triangle float64 for the statistic, unless marked otherwise). The 524,288-
byte figure that recurs below is areferencecolumn: the n-CLU of the earlier composition-only
design was pinned there, and read-path-matched baselines carry about 131,072 float32 parameters
at it. It is not a limit the deletable object meets.
Baselines: naive against naive.Learned baselines (MLP, LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber,
1997), transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), and a continuous-input MLP) are matched to the n-
CLU’sread path, and fitted from the same frozen features. AtK ⋆ the fitted baselines carry 130,433
to 131,891 parameters. The memory footprint is not a read-path parameter; it is printed, never
matched (Borgeaud et al., 2022; Khandelwal et al., 2020; Lample et al., 2019). A lookup table of
written items is a printedfloor, never a baseline. Four controls, which we call thepre-declared
landscape controlsthroughout,landscape removed(atoms zeroed),landscape shuffled(atom values
permuted within each well),shuffled across wells(well blocks dealt to other wells) andrandom
atoms(random atoms in place of the fitted ones), must collapse before any row is claimed for the
n-CLU; if they do not, the row is an interface row or nothing.
The judge and the estimand.On Shapes3D an external classifier, hash-pinned per seed, judges
each rendered image; the metric is the number ofdistinct valid novel factor tuples per held-out
combination, a count, with ceilings of 36 (pair combinations) and 72 (six-factor combinations). The
estimand is paired over combinations with a margin of 1.0 tuple per held-out combination declared
before any number. Where an experiment declared its success and falsification criteria before its
first number, we refer to each as adeclared criterion, labelled as in that declaration (for example,
criterion S1 or criterion S2). Seed count is a column of every table.

retrain reads 120,028 to 129,761 rows and takes 547.7 to 2,064.5 s. After the value and item deletions
the n-CLU stays ahead of every retrained baseline on every seed, by +6.954 to +32.607 tuples per
held-out combination with every 95% interval above zero. After the red-sphere deletion it is ahead
on the mean at every seed, but its interval crosses zero against the MLP at seeds 1 and 2 (+2.486 and

3.5 REFUSAL AFTER DELETION:THE HOLE,A SEPARATE MECHANISM
Refusal is a second mechanism on the same object: a coupling table withholes. Punching a hole for
a deleted value or conjunction marks the request slot as refused. On theK ⋆ object two holes cost 2
bytes, read 0 data rows, take 0.0000 s and refit nothing, in a 1,322-byte coupling table whose layout
is asserted equal to the field-mode n-CLU’s over 1,322 pair slots. One hole (red∧cube) is refusable
although no red cube was ever written: refusal does not need a deleted row to have existed. The
hole is the only mechanism on this object that refuses, and it is a flag: a request for a holed value is
refused by construction, so refusal is measurement (c) of Tab. 4, a display row, and symbolic refusal
is not claimed. An item deletion makes nothing impossible: on the n-CLU’s own 131,072-row pool,
deleting a random 1% of items clears no value bit and punches no hole, so no request becomes
certifiably impossible and the item row has no refusal row at all.

This section asks the n-CLU togeneratea world model’s transitions on a colour maze, under the

CMAZE-T, a minor contribution: a small world-model task for composition.We offer the
transition instrument to researchers working on composition, as a small task and not as a benchmark.
Given a colour-maze state and a move, an arm generates the next state; the answer is four facts
(legality, displacement, event, pocket) and the engine’s judge scores it with no tolerance (Fig. 2). Test
mazes are generated at random, so a lookup of training mazes cannot transfer, and the cross-maze
lookup floors are printed. The request is five words; the held-out families are a never-trained value,
unseen two-way word pairs and unseenn-way word combinations; the judge accepts or refuses
validity; learned blind arms that drop the interaction terms or the colour identity are printed as
falsifiers. The task is small, its results differ widely from seed to seed, and it has one engine; those
are reasons to report it per seed, not to rank systems on it.
CMAZE-T with symmetric inputs: six seeds.Tab. 20 gives every input condition, and within
a condition every arm receives the identical input. The n-CLU here is the base design (latent 128,

Reading the CMAZE-T panel figures.Figs. 10–13 (App. N) draw single held-out transitions
with every model’s next state. Each panel is a5×5crop of the maze centred on the agent’s cell at
st. Walls are dark, tinted cells pale in their colour, doors framed in their colour with a bar across
while closed, untaken keys are diamonds, the goal a cross, and the agent a black disc whose centre
shows the colour of the key in its pocket (white when the pocket is empty). The first column is
st with the action (an arrow, or a ring for pick-up), the second the engine’ss t+1, and each further
column thes t+1 that a model’s emitted outcome produces. The engine’s judge does not score the
picture: it scores four emitted facts, legality, the move, the key event and the pocket after the move,
and a picture need not show all four. So a strip under everys t+1 prints the emitted facts (under the
engine’s column, the true ones). Its first line reads legal or illegal. Its second line shows the move
(↑→↓←, or a dot for staying), the key event (+and a colour for a key picked up,−and a colour
for a key used at a door, blank for none) and the pocket after the move (the disc; white is empty).
An illegal emission leaves the agent in place and is scored on its pocket alone, so only the pocket is
shown. The tick or cross on a panel is the judge’s verdict on all four facts, so two panels that show
the same picture and the same strip never carry opposite marks.

5.2 THE CLUE:THE N-CLUIS THE ZERO-DYNAMICS LIMIT OF A SETTLING UNIT
The n-CLU we evaluate is the zero-dynamics limit of the CLU: the same content, the same wells,
the same read, with the settling loop’s step count set to zero. Inside a well this is an algebraic
identity: the n-CLU’s energy is the distance to the atom span, the request’s basin sits at an address
inside that span, so both minima coincide and an in-well settle has nowhere to go (derivation in
App. J). We asserted this in code: the zero-step arm was required to be bit-identical to the static
read bynp.array equalover 256 requests, and it was, on all three scored seeds, returning the
static read’s held-out scores to every printed digit (row 4: 63.612 / 64.327 / 67.653; row 3: 29.717
/ 31.617 / 31.450). The only place dynamics can act is across wells: with a bilinear low-rank
couplingλWthe settled state isz ⋆ = (βI−λW) −1βzreq, which returnsz req exactly atλ= 0.
Switching the dynamics on lowers composition at every strength at which they move the state:
−0.531/−0.510/−0.388six-factor tuples per held-out combination at the gentlest setting that
moves it (median displacement 0.009), and−39.959/−46.918/−39.061at full strength (median
displacement 0.39), while incurring 81,312 to 84,216 bytes of read-side coupling that the static read
does not. The identity holds under four named conditions (App. J).

5.3 THE THEORY WE CARRY:THELATENTDYNAMICSMODEL CLASS
We call the class of objects that this n-CLU is the static limit of aLatent Dynamics Model: a memory
whose content is read by relaxing a latent state under a designed energy (Hopfield, 1982; Krotov &
Hopfield, 2016; Ramsauer et al., 2021; Hoover et al., 2023; Kong et al., 2024), of which the n-CLU
is the member with the relaxation switched off. (The phrase is used in reduced-order modelling and
in model-based reinforcement learning for a learned surrogate of an environment’s dynamics; here
the dynamics are the read, not a model of the data.) Its laws are this paper’s own theory, each with a
status label and rider (further laws in App. L).
Two laws measured on this study’s own n-CLU (CONFIRMED[THIS STUDY]).Switched on,
the field-mode n-CLU’s relaxation obeys a curvature–retention relationship whose prediction is a
time, not a score: with nothing fitted, it predicts the settle half-life to a median factor of 1.105 on
49 held-out combinations and three seeds (bar 0.10 on the log error; 0.0433 / 0.0435 / 0.0431), and
beats a one-parameter form fitted with sight of the held-out measurements on 49 of 49 combinations
on every seed; one of six declared criteria (the V-curve argmin) misses on all three seeds, and the
law predicts where its simplest form stops. Deletion commutes with scheduled decay on a learned
memory, 0 bytes differing in a 2,054,866-byte image, while the stronger claim that the post-delete
memory equals the never-inserted one isREFUTED(65.17% to 65.39% of the image differs, 83.5%
of it because the address book and coupling table are fitted on the pool).These are dynamics results
about our own n-CLU. They occupy no row of the evaluation, they beat no model, and we make no
capability claim from them; no arm of ours fails the retention law, so it distinguishes nothing. The
full statements, criteria and misses are in App. K.
5.4 LEAVING NOTE
The naive unit provides deletion by subtraction, matching an n-CLU never fitted on the deleted rows,
and, at a stated partition, composition on Shapes3D that scores above read-path-matched baselines
fitted on its own rows, by about seven tuples per held-out combination over the strongest. On
the maze transition task its lead over matched baselines is small and seed-dependent, and its best
configuration is a request coding any learner can be given; deleting a conjunction removes what the
book knew about the shared value; and it incurs the memory and time cost of that match, in bytes
and in the book’s refit. Switched on, the dynamics lower composition at every strength that moves
the state, while a law that no arm of ours fails predicts their relaxation: so far alaw about the n-CLU,
not alever on the task. The experiment that would make the law a claim about a class, a comparator
predicted in advance to be mispredicted in theh/h ⋆∈[0.5,2]bucket, is named and not run.

ETHICS STATEMENT
The study uses a public synthetic image dataset (Shapes3D) and procedurally generated mazes; it
involves no human subjects and no personal data. Deletion is relevant to data-protection requests;
we claim only that the n-CLU and its address book match those never fitted on the deleted rows
(the frozen encoder and decoder are outside the object) and no certified unlearning, and we state in
Sec. 3.3 a case in which deleting a conjunction changes what the model answers about the values
that survive it.

The four conditions.(C1) The n-CLU’s energy is distance to the atom span, the atoms being a
basis: with an atoms-as-points energy on the same content the settle moves (relative displacement

L PRIOR DERIVATIONS
The laws below are this paper’s own theory. Each carries a status:CONFIRMED[THIS STUDY]
where it was measured on this study’s own n-CLU;CONFIRMED[PRIOR DERIVATION] where it
was derived and validated on designed or small trained testbeds outside this study’s n-CLU and was
not re-run here;REFUTEDandUNTESTEDas stated. Status discipline is part of the section.
Memory-level deletion in a designed memory (CONFIRMED[PRIOR DERIVATION]).With an
order-independent waitlist, memory-level deletion leaves a memory byte-equal to one never written
with the deleted items, at every load measured, 0.29 to 1.71 times lattice capacity, a membership
signal of AUC 0.5000±0.0000 on all six statistics and byte equality 1.0000, under three stated
conditions (capacity at least the number of cells, zero leak, priority- or attribute-based eviction;
recency-based eviction is excluded as intrinsically history-dependent). The placement rule is the
Blelloch–Golovin stable-matching table (Blelloch & Golovin, 2007) with a global priority order;
the theorems follow from theirs and the fix-up cascade is theirs. Its scoping is the one this paper
adopts in Sec. 2: an n-CLU-level guarantee only, the frozen encoder and any residue of past writes
in a learned landscape being separate channels, and no certified(ε,δ)unlearning claimed.
The damping optimum (CONFIRMED[PRIOR DERIVATION];REFUTEDas a rollout prediction
on this n-CLU).On the exact one-step Jacobian the emergent retention half-life is a V-curve in
the damping, with its argmin at0.902±0.003ofγ crit and slopes−1.0020and+1.116on three of
three emergent seeds, one damping-optimum curve at two values of the curvature rather than two
laws. It holds only with fluctuation–dissipation-consistent noise and a Newtonian kinetic term. On
this study’s field-mode n-CLU the rollout misses the same argmin on all three seeds (above), with
the failing step named: the pure-exponential envelope is invalid at the defective point.
