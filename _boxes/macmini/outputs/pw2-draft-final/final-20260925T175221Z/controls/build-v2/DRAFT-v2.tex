\begin{abstract}
A world model trusted with requests it has never seen needs three things at once: its outputs are \emph{valid} under the task's judge, it \emph{composes}
held-out combinations of factors from factors it has seen, and it can \emph{forget} specific
training rows. Compositional generation is a crowded area
and machine unlearning is an established one. We work at the level of a single \emph{naive unit} and
compare it only with other naive units (an MLP, an LSTM, a transformer and a continuous-input MLP):
we do not claim the best compositional-generation or unlearning system, and systems built on top of
units, such as sharded retraining or compositional diffusion, are out of scope.
We study the simplest object that has all three properties, the \emph{naive-CLU} (n-CLU): a
label-free set of per-well principal subspaces computed on the latents of a frozen
encoder, read by a request-indexed address book and rendered by a frozen decoder, whose
content is derived from a retained sufficient statistic. Deleting rows downdates the
statistic, not the truncated factors, so the n-CLU after deletion equals the n-CLU fitted
without those rows. On a Shapes3D value hold-out, at the finest partition on a validation plateau
that the seeds do not resolve, % SRC: w15-crossover-seeds/CROSSOVER-3SEED.md
the n-CLU composes held-out pairs and held-out combinations of every factor, with every landscape
control collapsing. % SRC: w12b-nr6-and-kstar/NR6-T7.md

\subsection{Positioning among naive units}
\label{sec:positioning}

The naive-CLU (n-CLU) is a \emph{naive AI unit}: one model with no system built around it, in the sense
in which an MLP, an LSTM~\citep{hochreiter_long_1997} or a transformer~\citep{vaswani_attention_2023} is a unit. We compare it only with other naive units, an
MLP, an LSTM, a transformer and a continuous-input MLP, matched on the read path
(Sec.~\ref{sec:object}). We claim neither the best compositional-generation system nor the best
unlearning system. Systems built on top of units, such as sharded retraining, compositional
diffusion and physics-informed variants of an MLP, are out of scope by definition; each is cited
in one sentence below, with the unit/system distinction stated.

Generating held-out combinations of factors is an old and active problem~\citep{du_compositional_2020,avidan_compositional_2022}. The multilinear
line (\citealp{tenenbaum_separating_2000};
TensorFaces~\citep{goos_multilinear_2002}; \citealp{vlasic_face_2005}; MLC-VAE~\citep{georgopoulos_multilinear_2020}) factorises a data tensor organised by every label combination and
renders linearly; the frozen-generator line (GANSpace~\citep{harkonen_ganspace_2020}; TCA~\citep{oldfield_tensor_2021}; PandA~\citep{oldfield_panda_2023}, and its
2024 journal version~\citep{oldfield_bilinear_2024}) finds editable directions in a pretrained generator without labels and
without an address book. We do not claim either half. In particular we do not claim
multilinear or bilinear factored generation, a request-indexed tensor head, tensor-product
label conditioning, or the downdate primitive, which follows in one line from the summation
form of~\citet{cao_towards_2015} because a covariance is a summation.

Unlearning that matches a retrain without the deleted rows is also established, for $k$-means~\citep{ginart_making_2019}, for bilinear
matrix completion~\citep{xu_netflix_2023}, and for ridge heads on frozen backbones~\citep{ceci_exact_2027}, where the most recent work
restricts itself explicitly to ridge regression and excludes principal-component,
eigendecomposition and generative models. The nearest generative attempts are approximate~\citep{kong_approximate_2022}:
feature unlearning for pretrained GANs and VAEs~\citep{moon_feature_2024} unlearns a feature rather than rows and
fine-tunes the generator; data redaction from pretrained GANs~\citep{kong_data_2023} states that redaction is a
different task from deletion. GAN unlearning by substitution~\citep{sun_generative_2025} is approximate by construction.

A systematic screen of both literatures records what we could find. A forward-citation
crawl~\citep{priem_openalex_2022} over every title and abstract citing the canonical works of either side screened
about four thousand unique works: those citing the generation-side anchors, those citing the
deletion-side anchors, those from orthogonal queries that cite neither, and those from
a final targeted sweep. % SRC: _thinkpad/w8/w8-novelty/NOVELTY.md
Deletion language appears in ten of the generation-side works, most of them
false positives and none deleting training rows; no generation-side work combines frozen-decoder,
tensor-head and label-index language; the highest number of the six claim
atoms reached by any single work is four; and of the deletion-side works none combines
principal-subspace, generative and frozen-model language. % SRC: _thinkpad/w8/w8-novelty/NOVELTY.md
% PW2-5 moved -> pointer only (no table carries the screen): approximately 4,050 unique works; 3,038 generation-side; 759 deletion-side; 254 from 21 orthogonal queries; about 100 from a final sweep; deletion language in 10 of 3,038 (0.3%), seven false positives; 0 of 3,038; 4 of 6 claim atoms; 759 deletion-side works; SRC: _thinkpad/w8/w8-novelty/NOVELTY.md
The orthogonal query that asks the question directly, \emph{compositional} $\wedge$
\emph{generative} $\wedge$ a deletion phrase, returns zero works on the screen.
% SRC: _thinkpad/w8/w8-novelty/NOVELTY.md
These are the screen's counts. Abstract-level screening
cannot see a match buried in a method section, and indexing of preprints younger than about
three months is incomplete (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}).
Three families of systems built on top of units are out of scope.
SISA training~\citep{bourtoule_machine_2021} is a system that makes any unit, a generator included,
unlearnable in this sense by splitting its data into shards and retraining the unit on the shard that
held the deleted rows, which reads that shard's retained data; we compare with the unit it wraps,
whose only route to that match is the retrain of Sec.~\ref{sec:del-comparators}.
Compartmentalized diffusion models~\citep{golatkar_training_2024} are a system that
trains separate diffusion models on separate data sources, composes them at inference and forgets
a source by dropping its model, so what is composed is models of data sources.
% cite-tagger: bib entry added by w20 citation-prune -- golatkar2023training (Golatkar, Achille, Swaminathan, Soatto, "Training Data Protection with Compositional Diffusion Models", arXiv:2308.01937)
Physics-informed variants of an MLP, such as physics-informed neural
networks~\citep{raissi_physics-informed_2019} and Hamiltonian neural networks~\citep{greydanus_hamiltonian_2019}, are
systems that add a physical loss or structure to an MLP unit, and the baseline we compare with is the
plain MLP unit.
% cite-tagger: bib entries added by w20 citation-prune -- raissi2019physics (Raissi, Perdikaris, Karniadakis, "Physics-informed neural networks", J. Comput. Phys. 378, 2019); greydanus2019hamiltonian (Greydanus, Dzamba, Yosinski, "Hamiltonian Neural Networks", NeurIPS 2019)

One distinction is ours. The decremental-PCA line
(\citealp{hall_adding_2002}, and successors~\citep{jin_evd_2014,lee_fast_2021}) subtracts \emph{deflated} eigenspaces; its
authors describe the result as approximating the refit, report that subtraction tends to
instability, and give no rigorous error analysis. Our route matches the refit.
\emph{Downdate the statistic, not the truncated factors}. The full, untruncated second-moment
statistic is downdated by subtraction and the content is re-eigendecomposed afterwards, so no
truncation error ever enters the subtraction.

\subsection{The three properties}
\label{sec:why}

A world model~\citep{ha_world_2018,hafner_mastering_2025} is useful to the extent that it can be asked for states and transitions it
has not seen~\citep{valevski_diffusion_2025}, and trusted with the answer. We name three properties and use them throughout. PLANTED Sec.~\ref{sec:planted-nowhere} and~\citep{planted_nokey_2099}.
\textbf{Rule validity}: an output is valid if the task's judge accepts it (on the maze, the engine's judge, which uses
integer arithmetic with no tolerance)~\citep{vafa_evaluating_2024}; approximate similarity to a training item does not count.
\textbf{Composition}: the model produces valid outputs for held-out combinations of factor
values that it has seen only separately~\citep{zhao_toward_2022}. \textbf{Deletion}: after a set of training rows
is deleted, the deletable object matches, to floating-point roundoff, an object that was never fitted
on those rows. The three pull against each other: a lookup table deletes in this sense but composes
nothing it has not written; a learned generator composes but meets this definition only by retraining
(the whole model, or a shard of it~\citep{bourtoule_machine_2021}); and
approximate unlearning~\citep{guo_certified_2023,golatkar_eternal_2020} is approximate by construction and does not meet it (Sec.~\ref{sec:del-comparators}).
The object we study makes the three properties of one n-CLU, and we measure, row by row, where each
holds and where it does not.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:object}
% =======================================================================================

% PW2 item 3 (PLAN.md sec. 2): the Head's Methods opening, verbatim; LaTeX only (\citet = "Jawahar & Pierini (2026)").
\citet{jawahar2026chlu} introduced the CHLU as a latent dynamics model (defined here as an algorithm
that either enforces or extracts the dynamics of the latent space itself to either design or
approximately understand semantic rules created by the encoder that produced this abstract space
such that downstream tasks can be appropriately dealt with or downstream design choices can be
better informed). \citet{jawahar2026chlu} envision their unit (as the name suggests) as a step that
provides a clue towards developing better latent dynamics models. We build on top of this idea and
drop the complex physics design to construct our naive-CLU as a special case of the CHLU and test it
as a factorized memory unit for compositional generation with additional properties such as deletion
as a virtue of the design itself.

A claim about any of the three properties is only as precise as the definitions of the object,
its judge and its memory footprint. We therefore fix these here, before any result.

\paragraph{The n-CLU.} A frozen encoder maps each training item to a latent. The latent
space is cut, without labels, into $K$ wells by a balanced correlation partition; in each well
the n-CLU keeps the leading principal subspace of the fit pool, with the pool mean as the
first atom. No label enters the content. We write $n$ for the number of factors jointly
composed by a request and $K$ for the number of wells, and never write both as $k$.

\paragraph{The read.} A request is a tuple of factor values. A tensor-product address book,
fitted on the fit pool, maps the request to per-well coefficients; the read renders
$x_k=U_k c_k$ in each well's orthonormal basis and the frozen decoder renders the image. The
read is request-only. No feature tensor enters it, which is asserted on the deployed object
(bitwise stable under feature perturbation over 64 checks at $\Kstar$).
% SRC: w13-seeds-and-tables/TABLES.md
Labels therefore touch the model only at the address book. This is the interface split:
unsupervised content, a request-indexed head, and a frozen renderer, with no Cartesian-product
training requirement~\citep{wiedemer_compositional_2023,lachapelle_additive_2023,montero_lost_2022}.

\paragraph{The memory footprint.} Every n-CLU row prints four byte counts
separately: (1) the atoms, (2) the covariance and count statistic, (3) the address book, and
(4) the coupling and hole table used for refusal. Every
byte figure names its convention (symmetric-triangle float64 for the statistic, unless marked
otherwise). The 524,288-byte figure that recurs below is a \emph{reference} column: the n-CLU
of the earlier composition-only design was pinned there, and read-path-matched baselines carry
about 131,072 float32 parameters at it. % SRC: w13-seeds-and-tables/TABLES.md
It is not a limit the object meets.

% pw1-draft-figures (item 1.5 / 8): removed the per-seed ceiling paragraph (ceiling values, unsupported-row counts, cap), replaced by the hold-out mechanism; its numbers stay in: w21-maze-t-seed4/FINDINGS.md; w21-maze-t-seed4/TABLES.md; w21-words-full-test/FINDINGS.md; w21-rel-newseeds/FINDINGS.md
% SRCBLOCK: w21-maze-t-seed4/FINDINGS.md
\paragraph{Colour relations under the hold-out draw.} The hold-out removes word
combinations from training at random. On some draws, for some colour, it removes every training
transition in which the pocket and the target tint share that colour, the combination that decides
whether a move is legal. A model that codes colour by identity then has no training evidence for the
held-out transitions of that colour and cannot answer them, however it is trained. A model that is
also given the relation ``pocket colour equals tint colour'' can: the relation is learned from the
other colours, where it is seen, and applies unchanged to the colour whose rows were removed. This is
what the relational columns add (Fig.~\ref{fig:maze-wm}(c)), and it is why every seed is reported
separately.
% ENDSRC

\paragraph{The judge and the estimand.} On Shapes3D an external classifier, hash-pinned per
seed, judges each rendered image; the metric is the number of \emph{distinct valid novel
factor tuples per held-out combination}, a count, with a fixed ceiling for pair and for six-factor
combinations (Tab.~\ref{tab:t7}). The estimand is paired over combinations with a margin of 1.0 tuple per held-out combination declared
before any number. % SRC: w13-seeds-and-tables/TABLES.md
% PW2-5 moved -> Tab. t7 caption (ceiling 36 on row 3, 72 on row 4): 36; 72; SRC: w13-seeds-and-tables/TABLES.md
Where an experiment declared its success and falsification criteria before its first
number, we refer to each as a \emph{declared criterion}, labelled as in that
declaration (for example, criterion S1 or criterion S2).

\paragraph{The retained statistic and deletion.} Beside the content the n-CLU keeps, per
well, the running covariance, mean and count of the fit rows. Deleting rows subtracts their
contribution from the statistic. The atoms are re-eigendecomposed from the downdated
statistic. The address book keeps its own retained statistic, per request tuple the count and first
moment of its fit rows; deleting rows downdates it, and the book is refit from it. Neither step
reads a training row, but the book's statistic is as large as its fit data
(Sec.~\ref{sec:shapes-del}). We use one definition of deletion throughout, and state its scope
before any table:

\begin{quote}
\textsc{The match is on the n-CLU and its address book; the frozen encoder and decoder are outside
the object.} After deleting a set of rows, the n-CLU matches an n-CLU that was never fitted on the
deleted rows, and the address book likewise: content and book equal, to float roundoff, those fitted
from the retained rows alone. Neither frozen network is refit at any point. It is
not retrain-equivalence of the generative model, it is not a claim that the renderer forgets, and it
is not a claim that the answers on the surviving requests are unchanged.
% SRC: w20-shapes-data-matched/FINDINGS.md
\end{quote}

\noindent The frozen encoder and decoder are declared outside the deletable object: they are
frozen, so no deletion touches their weights, which may have been fitted with the deleted rows
present.

The scoping sentence we carry is the one our deletion theory states for its own n-CLU: this is
an n-CLU-level guarantee only; the frozen encoder and any residue of past writes in a learned
landscape are separate channels, measured separately. We do not claim certified
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$ unlearning~\citep{guo_certified_2023}. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md

\subsection{The deletion protocol}

Three deletions are run on the $\Kstar$ object on TEST, on its fit pool (Tab.~\ref{tab:del}), at three % SRC: w20-shapes-data-matched/FINDINGS.md
% PW2-5 moved -> Tab. del caption (fit pool 131,072 rows): 131,072; SRC: w20-shapes-data-matched/FINDINGS.md
seeds. \emph{Value}: every fit row carrying red. \emph{Conjunction}: every fit row carrying red
\emph{and} sphere; we call it the red sphere everywhere. The conjunction first named in the design,
red $\wedge$ cube, deletes no rows, because cube is the shape held out of the split entirely; it is
printed as vacuous and never as a deletion result. \emph{Item}: a random 1\% of fit rows, which is
also the control for refit instability. \emph{Forgetting $c$} is three operations on one object:
downdate the n-CLU's content; downdate the address book's statistic and refit the book from it; and punch the
hole for $c$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:hole}). Every deletion row therefore prints four measurements
(Tab.~\ref{tab:del}): (a) the n-CLU's content equals the content refit without the rows, a result; (b) the book
equals the book refit without the rows, a result; (c) requests for $c$ are refused through the hole,
a display row only, because the hole is a flag and symbolic refusal is not claimed; and (d)
composition on the surviving combinations before and after, a result, with the check that the downdated
object and the object refit without the rows compose identically.

\paragraph{A small world-model task for composition.} We offer the
transition instrument to researchers working on composition, as a small task and not as a benchmark.
Given a colour-maze state and a move, an arm generates the next state; the answer is four facts
(legality, displacement, event, pocket) and the engine's judge scores it with no tolerance
(Fig.~\ref{fig:task-cmazet}). Test mazes are generated at random, so a lookup of training mazes
cannot transfer, and the cross-maze lookup floors are printed. The request is five words. The
held-out families are a never-trained value, unseen two-way word pairs and unseen $n$-way word
combinations; the judge accepts or refuses validity; learned blind arms that drop the interaction
terms or the colour identity are printed as falsifiers. The task is small, its results differ widely from seed to seed, and it has one engine; those are
reasons to report it per seed.

\paragraph{The inference note.} \emph{We beat the naive fair baselines at
every $n$; the n-CLU's own rise from 2-way to 6-way is not to be inferred, because rungs 3--6
probe the same combinations and differ in fit-pool coverage.} Concretely,
rungs $n=3,4,5,6$ probe the identical combinations and rows; what varies is how
coarsely the hold-out is carved, and with it the held-out row count. Every arm fitted on the rung's own pool tracks that count closely,
inversely, and every arm not fitted on it is exactly constant (Tab.~\ref{tab:ladder}).

\subsection{The zero-dynamics clue}
\label{sec:clue}

The n-CLU we evaluate is the zero-dynamics limit of the CLU~\citep{jawahar2026chlu}: the same content, wells and read, with the settling loop's step count set to zero. Inside a well this is an
algebraic identity: the n-CLU's energy is the distance to the atom span, the request's basin sits
at an address inside that span, so both minima coincide and an in-well settle has nowhere to go
(derivation in App.~\ref{app:clue}). We asserted this in code. The zero-step arm was required to be bit-identical to
the static read by \texttt{np.array\_equal} over 256 requests, and it was, on all three scored
seeds, returning the static read's held-out scores to every printed digit. % SRC: w12-clue-theorist/CLUE.md
% PW2-5 moved -> pointer only (the same values print as the composition-only n-CLU row of Tab. ablations, 63.6122 / 64.3265 / 67.6531 and 29.7167 / 31.6167 / 31.4500; no table states the zero-step identity): row 4 63.612 / 64.327 / 67.653; row 3 29.717 / 31.617 / 31.450; SRC: w12-clue-theorist/CLUE.md

\subsection{The Latent Dynamics Model class}
\label{sec:theory}

We call the class of objects that this n-CLU is the static limit of a \emph{Latent Dynamics
Model}: a memory whose content is read by relaxing a latent state under a designed energy~\citep{hopfield_neural_1982,krotov_dense_2016,ramsauer_hopfield_2021,hoover_energy_2023,kong_reservoir-computing_2024}, of
which the n-CLU is the member with the relaxation switched off. (The phrase is used in
reduced-order modelling and in model-based reinforcement learning for a learned surrogate of an
environment's dynamics. Here the dynamics are the read.) Its laws are this paper's own theory, each with a status label and rider (further laws in App.~\ref{app:shorts}).

\paragraph{Two laws measured on this study's own n-CLU.}
Switched on, the field-mode n-CLU's relaxation obeys a curvature--retention relationship whose
prediction is a \emph{time}: with nothing fitted, it predicts the settle half-life
well within its declared bar on the held-out combinations and three seeds (Tab.~\ref{tab:crr}), and beats a one-parameter form fitted with sight of the held-out measurements
on every combination on every seed; one of six declared criteria (the V-curve argmin) misses on
all three seeds, and the law predicts where its simplest form stops.
% SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md
Deletion commutes with scheduled decay on a learned memory, no byte differing in the memory
image, while the stronger claim that the post-delete memory equals the never-inserted one is
\textsc{refuted} (nearly two thirds of the image differs, most of it because the address book
and coupling table are fitted on the pool; App.~\ref{app:laws}). % SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md
% PW2-5 moved -> Tab. crr (B1 0.0433 / 0.0435 / 0.0431 against 0.10; B2 49 of 49; 49 combinations x 3 seeds), App. laws prose (1.105; 2,054,866-byte image; 65.17% to 65.39%; 83.5%): 1.105; 49; 0.10; 0.0433 / 0.0435 / 0.0431; 49 of 49; 0 bytes; 2,054,866; 65.17% to 65.39%; 83.5%; SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md
\emph{These are dynamics results about our own n-CLU. We make no capability claim from them}; no arm of ours fails the retention law,
so it distinguishes nothing. The full statements, criteria and misses are in
App.~\ref{app:physics}.

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:leaving}

The n-CLU provides deletion by subtraction, matching an n-CLU never fitted on the deleted rows, and, at a stated partition, composition on
Shapes3D that scores above read-path-matched baselines fitted on its own rows, by about seven tuples per
held-out combination over the strongest. On the maze transition task its lead over matched
baselines is small and seed-dependent, and its best configuration is a request coding any learner can be
given; deleting a conjunction removes what the book knew about the shared value; and it incurs the
memory and time cost of that match, in bytes and in the book's refit. Switched on, the dynamics lower composition at
every strength that moves the state, while a law that no arm of ours fails predicts their relaxation:
so far a \emph{law about the n-CLU}, not a \emph{lever on the task}. The experiment that would make
the law a claim about a class, a comparator predicted in advance to be mispredicted in the
$h/h^\star\in[0.5,2]$ bucket, is named and not run. % SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md

\subsection{Physics follow-up}
\label{sec:followup}

The follow-up is a full evaluation of the settling (physics) CLU on the three properties of
Sec.~\ref{sec:why}, same judges, memory footprint and matching, with this paper's n-CLU as its zero-dynamics
ablation. What it must contain is fixed by this study's negatives (App.~\ref{app:bench}).

\paragraph{Deletion.} Improving deletion is also future work, and the measured comparison is
the clue: after deletion the n-CLU composes above every baseline retrained without the same rows on
the value and item scopes, and level with the MLP and the LSTM on the red sphere, where its 95\% % SRC: w20-shapes-data-matched/SHAPES-MATCHED.md
interval against them includes zero on some seeds (Sec.~\ref{sec:del-comparators}). A deletion that
removed the conjunction's rows without removing what the address book knows about the shared value
would close that gap.

\section{Reporting conventions}
\label{app:conventions}

\paragraph{Provenance.} Nearly every number in the source of this paper carries a comment naming
the record it was read from; the reproducibility statement gives the scope of the verbatim re-check. Numbers are quoted as the record prints them. None is re-derived.
Where two records print the same quantity at different precision, the one cited is the one the
number was taken from.

\paragraph{Two byte conventions, and which object carries which.} The statistic of every object
in the main text is counted under the symmetric-triangle float64 convention,
$\sum_k d_k(d_k+1)/2\times8$ B plus means and counts; the $\Kstar$ object's covariance is
549,120 B on it, which closes to the byte. % SRC: w13-seeds-and-tables/TABLES.md; w13-referee-2/REFEREE-v1.md
One object is counted under the full-square convention, $8\sum_k d_k^2$: the $K=5$ n-CLU of the
ladder, on the label-derived partition. We name the two $K=5$ objects apart everywhere:
\emph{$K=5$ (MI partition, full square)}, the ladder's n-CLU, well widths 777 / 936 / 216 / 60 /
59 on seed 0, statistic 12,284,920 B; and \emph{$K=5$ (ward, triangle)}, the crossover and
bridge n-CLU, five wells of width 409--410, statistic 3,391,552 B. They differ in partition and
in convention. The convention alone is a factor of about two.
% SRC: w13-referee-2/REFEREE-v1.md; w12b-nr6-and-kstar/FINDINGS.md; w13-seeds-and-tables/TABLES.md
The ladder's footprint record carries three parts; its coupling table is a declared absence, not a
zero. % SRC: w13-seeds-and-tables/TABLES.md

\paragraph{Seeds and configurations.} Seed count and configuration count are separate fields. The
phrase ``nine seeds'' never applies: where nine legs appear they are three configurations times
three seeds inside one implementation (App.~\ref{app:impl}).

\section{The zero-dynamics identity: conditions and provenance}
\label{app:clue}

\paragraph{The derivation.} The content is an orthonormal basis $U_k$ of the
per-well atom span, and the read renders the request's address through that basis,
$x_k=U_k c_k$. The n-CLU's energy for a basis is the distance to its span,
$E_{\text{content},k}=\tfrac12\operatorname{dist}^2(x_k,\operatorname{span}G_k)$, zero everywhere
on the span; the request enters as a basin at the address, $E_{\text{req},k}=\tfrac12\beta\lVert
x_k-a_k\rVert^2$, zero at the address. Because $a_k\in\operatorname{span}(G_k)$ by construction
of the renderer, both terms attain their minima at the same point, so an in-well settle has
nowhere to go. 

\paragraph{The four conditions.} (C1) The n-CLU's energy is distance to the atom span, the atoms
being a basis: with an atoms-as-points energy on the same content the settle moves (relative
displacement median 1.7244, max 2.6682 on a synthetic check). (C2) The address lies in the span:
pushed off the span by $\delta=0.10$ the settle moves by exactly $\delta/(1+\beta)$, measured
0.050000 against the closed form 0.050000. (C3) The state is carried in span coordinates. The same
energy in channel coordinates drifts by \sci{7.447}{-08} relative, float32 rounding, so the paper
says ``identical'' of the channel form and ``bit-identical'' only of the span form. (C4) No
cross-well term: at $\lambda=0$ the displacement is exactly 0.0; at
$\lambda=10^{-4}$, $10^{-2}$, $0.05$, $0.2$ it is \sci{7.875}{-05}, \sci{7.882}{-03},
\sci{3.971}{-02}, \sci{1.720}{-01}. Two wells of the deployed n-CLU were full rank, where an in-well
settle is trivially a no-op. The coercivity certificate reproduces from first principles:
$\lambda^\star=0.25000000$ and a certificate minimum eigenvalue of $-1.50000000$ at the declared
$\lambda=1$, so the landscape is unbounded below at the declared strength and the used strength is
0.225000. % SRC: w12-clue-theorist/CLUE.md
The identity is in-well only; a $\lambda=0$, steps$>0$ arm was never run on the deployed n-CLU (the
synthetic check covers steps 0 / 1 / 10 / 1000); and the \sci{1.9}{-7} off-span residual is the
renderer's float32 rounding, the same size for the static n-CLU that never moves.
% SRC: w12-clue-theorist/CLUE.md

headline reproduces: 0.043256 / 0.043516 / 0.043102 and $-0.596831$ / $-0.579639$ / $-0.598172$,
49 of 49 on every seed; the integrator is the theory's map to \sci{1.884682}{-08}; the byte pin is
524,288 B on every seed. A finite-difference Hessian check reads \sci{3.519263}{-11} /
\sci{1.202332}{-10} / \sci{2.699430}{-11}. % SRC: w12-clue-theorist/CLUE.md

\section{Prior derivations}
\label{app:shorts}

The laws below
are this paper's own theory. Each carries a status: \textsc{confirmed [this study]} where it was
measured on this study's own n-CLU; \textsc{confirmed [prior derivation]} where it was derived and
validated on designed or small trained testbeds outside this study's n-CLU and was not re-run
here; \textsc{refuted} and \textsc{untested} as stated.


\paragraph{Memory-level deletion in a designed memory (\textsc{confirmed [prior
derivation]}).} With an order-independent waitlist, memory-level deletion leaves a memory
byte-equal to one never written with the deleted items, at every load measured, 0.29 to 1.71 times lattice capacity, a membership signal of AUC 0.5000 $\pm$ 0.0000 on all
six statistics and byte equality 1.0000, under three stated conditions (capacity at least the number
of cells, zero leak, priority- or attribute-based eviction. Recency-based eviction is excluded as
intrinsically history-dependent). % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
The placement rule is the~\citet{blelloch_strongly_2007} stable-matching table with a global priority order;
the theorems follow from theirs and the fix-up cascade is theirs. Its scoping is the one this
paper adopts in Sec.~\ref{sec:object}: an n-CLU-level guarantee only, the frozen encoder and any
residue of past writes in a learned landscape being separate channels, and no certified
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$ unlearning claimed. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md

\paragraph{The damping optimum (\textsc{confirmed [prior derivation]}; \textsc{refuted} as a
rollout prediction on this n-CLU).} On the exact one-step Jacobian the emergent retention
half-life is a V-curve in the damping, with its argmin at $0.902\pm0.003$ of
$\gamma_{\text{crit}}$ and slopes $-1.0020$ and $+1.116$ on three of three emergent seeds, one
damping-optimum curve at two values of the curvature rather than two laws.
% SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
It holds only with fluctuation--dissipation-consistent noise and a Newtonian kinetic term. On
this study's field-mode n-CLU the rollout misses the same argmin on all three seeds (above), with the
failing step named: the pure-exponential envelope is invalid at the defective point.
% SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md

\paragraph{Interference is degree-bounded (\textsc{confirmed [prior derivation]}).} Cross-unit
basin displacement is exactly zero off the coupling graph (0 of 4656 off-graph entries nonzero
across 72 runs, three topologies), so interference is the degree times the per-edge displacement,
flat in the number of units at fixed degree, while a shared-potential monolith grows as
$N^{1.18\pm0.17}$. The firewall is parameter separation, not block structure.
% SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md

\paragraph{Asserted, not derived (\textsc{untested}).} Eight further statements of the theory,
including a lifetime trilemma whose consequence is measured, have no derivation and no
impossibility argument, and are carried as untested; a certificate family for reach and escape is
untested on this n-CLU, with its own measured limit that unit Jacobian determinant and zero
energy change are not sufficient for bounded dynamics (a certified excursion of 0.09 against
104.83 blind). % SRC: w13-draft-revise/DRAFT-v1.md; w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md
A law this study planned against, that a Mexican-hat width or separation sets the
interpolation floor and the refusal gap, does not exist in its own derivations, and the nearest
derived object inverts it. The retention floor is curvature-independent. % SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md


These results were derived and validated on designed or small trained testbeds before this study
and were not re-run on this study's n-CLU; their content enters here as this paper's own theory,
each with its rider. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Retention triad (qualitative).} A latch with infinite lifetime, a $\mu^{-2}$ scaling
law and bounded drift are qualitatively absent in learned recurrent baselines (median lifetime 5.6
  / 56 / 69 map-steps for three recurrent families; the emergent unit 263 steps, bounded 0.35 rad).
  Rider: a per-step compute factor of 6.2 against an LSTM inverts retention per unit of compute, so
  no ``longer'' claim is made on compute. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\item \textbf{GMOR~\citep{gell-mann_behavior_1968} on trained checkpoints.} With a linear ambient spurion the three objects satisfy
  $\mu^2F^2=\delta\Sigma$ to a maximum absolute deviation of \sci{1.33}{-15} on 8 trained SO(2)
  checkpoints. Rider: designed SO(2), probe-only, float64. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\item \textbf{Erosion law and anchor cure.} Contrastive wake--sleep training inverts a designed
  vacuum if and only if the flat direction is unconstrained by the wake phase; an anchor cures it,
  and every headline law survives past the erosion horizon under the cure. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\item \textbf{Friction preserves, temperature erases.} The coset diffusion law is verified to
  $1.0068\pm0.0219$ over 25 cells; damping 0.05 to 0.2 lengthens coset half-life
  $3.77\pm0.23$ times. Rider: two observables, two signs; a figure that does not name the initial
  condition is meaningless. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\item \textbf{The friction-hole vault.} $107.77\pm4.78$ times (the $\hat D$-estimator number; raw
  first-passage $86.97\pm2.94$), against a scalar friction of the same strength at
  $13.28\pm0.12$. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\item \textbf{Dissipative channel.} Sync time within 7.5\% of a registered prediction; the half-life
  claim is ranking-only; the retention law holds at $N\in\{2,4,8,16\}$. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\item \textbf{Reversible O(1)-memory training.} At zero damping, recompute-backwards training

  memory 6.3 KB against 6.00 MB at $T=1024$; exact only at zero damping. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\item \textbf{Hard read isolation (designed memory only).} A sub-barrier particle cannot read a
  foreign item; the predicted threshold mass 0.36 is bracketed by the sweep (crosses at 0.34,
  confines at 0.38). Rider: it does not transfer to a trained block. % SRC: shorts-evidence-inventory.md
\item \textbf{The trilemma.} With a quadratic payload channel, an anti-decoration guard and a fixed

\section{The encoder: adaptation and the unfreezing decomposition}
\label{app:encoder-main}

\subsection*{Light encoder adaptation (secondary)}

The frozen encoder is the primary object: a harness on top of a large encoder that is never
unfrozen. Light adaptation is optional. At $\Kstar$, three seeds,
equal steps and images across arms (400 steps, 25,600 images; encoder parameters in the
optimiser 0 / 131,072 / 428,576), the margin of the single design over the best learned
baseline grows from the frozen encoder under the \emph{anchor} arm (full unfreeze with a
penalty on distance from the frozen weights) on all three seeds: $+0.5688\to+1.3303$,
$-0.0275\to-0.0092$, $+1.1009\to+1.3028$. The low-rank \emph{adapter} grows it on two of
three ($+1.6239$, $+0.3670$, and $+0.3028$, a loss of $0.7982$ on seed 2).
% SRC: w13-encoder-adapt/CROSS-ARM.md
The comparator here is a 180k-parameter MLP, a different opponent from Tab.~\ref{tab:t7}, so
the two tables are never merged; a factor probe measured as a margin over random
initialisation changes nothing ($+0.0001$ to $-0.0160$ aggregate, per seed).
% SRC: w13-encoder-adapt/CROSS-ARM.md; w13-advisor-report.md

\subsection*{Unfreezing with the n-CLU on the gradient path: the decomposition}

An earlier CPU leg (shortened decoder, 400 joint steps, 8,192 training images, 3 seeds; a
different instrument, reported in its own table, App.~\ref{app:encloop}) asked whether
training the encoder through the n-CLU improves composition. The net is a wash because two
real effects cancel: unfreezing the encoder with the n-CLU on the gradient path hurts ($A'$
negative on 5 of 6 seed-rows) and a swap loss repairs that damage ($A''$ positive on 4 of 6;
the four two-sided sign-test $p$-values are 0.0001751, 0.000198, 0.000346 and 0.0001564, three
of them at or below 0.0003). This is a finding about the encoder. The claim
that the n-CLU shapes the representation is not made. % SRC: w13-seeds-and-tables/TABLES.md; w13-draft-revise/DRAFT-v1.md
A longer leg at ten times the steps (4,000 joint steps, 256,000 images per arm) corroborates
the direction of the swap-loss ingredient on its probe margin, $A''$ positive on all three
seeds ($+0.0100$ / $+0.0129$ / $+0.0075$), while its equal-seconds control is short by 41.2 /
27.8 / 18.6 s and is biased toward the treatment. % SRC: w8-BAR-D-fold.md
\paragraph{The decomposition re-stated on the longer leg.} On the
longer, full-decoder leg (Tab.~\ref{tab:bard-gpu}) the ordering is \textbf{not reproduced} by its
pre-declared rule, which asks for $A'<0$ and $A''>0$ each in at least four of six cells: $A'$ is

\section{The two laws on this study's n-CLU, stated in full}
\label{app:laws}

\paragraph{Retention on the n-CLU (\textsc{confirmed [this study]}).} Switched on, the field-mode
n-CLU's relaxation obeys a curvature--retention relationship (CRR) whose prediction is a
\emph{time}. On the damped velocity-Verlet map the n-CLU deploys, the slow
multiplier gives $n_{1/2}=\ln2/(-\ln\lvert\lambda_{\text{slow}}\rvert)$ with nothing fitted. On 49
held-out combinations and three seeds the median $\lvert\log_{10}(n_{\text{meas}}/n_{\text{pred}})\rvert$ is
0.0433 / 0.0435 / 0.0431 against a declared bar of 0.10, a factor of 1.105 at the median
combination, seed spread 0.0004; and it beats a one-parameter functional form \emph{fitted with sight
of the held-out measurements} on 49 of 49 combinations on every seed (mean paired $\Delta$ $-0.5968$ /
$-0.5796$ / $-0.5982$; that fitted form's own median error is 0.640132 / 0.623155 / 0.641281).
% SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md
On an exact quadratic, where the law holds by construction, the same measurement chain recovers it
only to a median of 0.0349 (p90 0.0749, max 0.1601 over 17 cells), so the 0.10 bar is about three
times the instrument's floor. % SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md
The law predicts where its own simplest form stops: the median error is 0.0008 at $h/h^\star<0.1$
and 0.1482 in the one bucket straddling the exceptional point, where the $2\times2$ map is
defective. % SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md
One of the six declared criteria missed on all three seeds: the V-curve argmin sits at 0.3362 /
0.3301 / 0.3520 of $\gamma_{\text{crit}}$ against a factor-2 bar, a miss declared with its
cause before the run; and the stricter reading of a second bar, its maximum over $\gamma$, is
not moved: 59.1158 / 0.1612 / 1883.2934 against 0.10.
% SRC: w10-physics-section-evidence/EVIDENCE.md
\emph{This is a dynamics result about our own n-CLU. We make no capability claim from it}; no arm of ours fails the law, so the law
distinguishes nothing, which is why it is theory and not a result row.

\paragraph{Deletion commutes with decay (\textsc{confirmed [this study]}) and its companion
(\textsc{refuted}).} On a learned memory with scheduled per-item decay,
$\text{evict}\circ\text{decay}^n=\text{decay}^n\circ\text{evict}$ with 0 bytes differing in a

of the obstruction and 83.5\% is that the address book and coupling table are fitted on the pool.
