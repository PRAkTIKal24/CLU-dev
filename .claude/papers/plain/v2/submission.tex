\title{The Price of a Flat Direction:\\ Transverse Curvature Sets Retention in a Trained Recurrent Memory}

\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle


\begin{abstract}
A recurrent state can store a continuous value on a manifold of marginally stable states, and this literature already knows that such a manifold is fragile: noise diffuses the state along it, most infinitesimal perturbations of the dynamical law destroy it, and corrective mechanisms that keep it alive are classic. \emph{What has not been available is the exchange rate.} We measure one, on a trained potential. The object is a recurrent unit advancing a latent state $(q,p)$ by a damped symplectic velocity-Verlet step of a learned Hamiltonian, whose trained potential $V_\theta$ is $G$-invariant with minimisers on a $G$-orbit, so that the curvature $\mu$ transverse to that orbit prices how long a written value survives. The price list has three parts, every number at latent dimension $4$ on an $S^1$ testbed, $\le5$ seeds, laptop CPU. Retention half-life scales as $n_{1/2}\propto\mu^{-2}$, at fitted overdamped slope $-0.985$ against a predicted $-1$, the underlying curvature identity exact to $1.000000\pm5\!\times\!10^{-12}$ over $4.5$ decades. The law stops at a crossover $\varepsilon\mu\approx\gamma/2$, past which the half-life saturates at a curvature-independent floor. And the whole list survives the training-time correction that keeps the orbit alive, at $\approx20\times$ the horizon on which the objective would otherwise destroy it. Its evidence on a learned system is a head-to-head: a recently posted machine-learning lifetime law, run unchanged on our trained checkpoints, is this price list's overdamped face --- matching to $\le3\%$ below the crossover and mispredicting by $\approx3.2\times$ above it, in the calculable direction. \emph{We therefore also report where the price list does not extend, and those boundaries are part of the result:} on this architecture class symmetric training data alone does not produce a near-flat direction, a gap of $13$--$14$ orders in $\mu^2$ that is a measurement on our recipe and never a general claim about learning; the continuous coset register is designed-only; the raw retention ratio against recurrent baselines inverts under compute normalization and is retired as a compute claim; and four positioning claims are retired against prior art. Designed-testbed results are labeled verification; learned-system results, evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A recurrent state holding an analog value can be built on a flat direction: a set of states the dynamics do not distinguish, so a written value neither decays nor is pulled to a preferred point. Symmetry supplies such directions for free. If a learned potential $V_\theta$ is $G$-invariant and its minimiser is not, the minimisers form a $G$-orbit, the Hessian annihilates every orbit tangent, and the orbit coordinate is neutral. \emph{However}, a flat direction is only as useful as its price list, and none existed for a trained model: how much retention a given transverse curvature buys, where that law stops holding, and whether it survives the correction that keeps the orbit alive. \emph{We measure all three.} The object advances $(q,p)$ by a symplectic velocity-Verlet step of a learnable separable Hamiltonian $H(q,p)=T(p)+V_\theta(q)$, with a per-step damping $p\mapsto(1-\gamma)p$ supplying controllable forgetting. This is not a new architecture but a closed-form account of retention on a trained one. The reference unit is the CLU (Causal Learning Unit), introduced as CHLU in Jawahar \& Pierini (2026); results hold for the class of damped symplectic recurrences, and the exactly-solvable theory verified here is developed in a separate note, cited as \emph{the theory note} (Anonymous, 2026, in the supplementary material).

\paragraph{Two masses, and one conversion.} Two quantities here are called ``mass'' and run in opposite directions. The inertial mass $M_i$ is the learned diagonal of $T$: larger $M$, slower. The spectral mass $\mu_k$, which we lead with as the \emph{transverse curvature}, is $\mu_k^2=\lambda_k(M^{-1/2}\nabla^2V_\theta M^{-1/2})$ at a critical point: larger $\mu$, shorter memory. All retention statements use $\mu$. One conversion precedes any cross-field comparison: flow-Jacobian eigenvalues are inverse time, whereas $\mu^2$ are eigenvalues of a mass-whitened Hessian of a \emph{potential}, inverse time squared. Lifetimes are half-lives $n_{1/2}$ in map applications, not $\tau$ and not diffusion coefficients $D$; each is reported with the metric defining it, because those metrics bifurcate past the crossover.

\paragraph{Contribution.} This paper makes one, and it is quantitative: \emph{the transverse-curvature price list of a trained recurrent memory} (\S\ref{sec:pricelist}), in three parts --- what a given curvature buys, where the law stops, and whether it survives the training-time correction. \emph{Its evidence on a learned system} is the head-to-head of \S\ref{sec:headtohead}: a recently posted lifetime law, run unchanged on our trained checkpoints, is the price list's overdamped face and fails above the crossover in the direction the list predicts. \emph{Its honest negative} is the boundary of \S\ref{sec:boundary}: on this architecture class the flat direction has to be designed rather than trained for, the continuous register does not transfer to an emergent potential, and the retention advantage over recurrent baselines inverts under compute normalization. Designed-testbed results --- invariant potentials, analytic tilts and spurions --- are verification of the theory's exactness; learned-system, training-dynamics and head-to-head results are evidence.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Two questions this audience is asking --- how neural representations behave \emph{dynamically}, and what symmetry buys a learning system --- meet in one object, and this paper is about the price of that object. Neutrality of group-orbit directions is classical (Golubitsky, Stewart \& Schaeffer 1988; Krupa 1990; Rumberger 2001). The same object is studied as a \emph{continuous attractor}: a manifold whose points are all fixed points, ``marginally stable tangent to the manifold and stable normal to the manifold'' (S\'agodi et al.\ 2024), the substrate of the neural integrator (Seung 1996) and of measured ring and toroidal codes (Kim et al.\ 2017; Gardner et al.\ 2022; Khona \& Fiete 2022). We make no claim about biological systems and do not model neural data.

\emph{Five facts about that object are established, and none of them is ours.} Noise diffuses the coordinate along the manifold, the diffusion coefficient lower-bounded by the code's own Fisher information (Burak \& Fiete 2012). The manifold is structurally unstable, destroyed by most infinitesimal changes of the dynamical law, with online learning named as one perturbation source --- the \emph{fine-tuning problem}, whose memory error S\'agodi et al.\ bound by the unknown perturbation's size, worst-case and linear in $t$. A homeostatic mechanism that restores a destroyed flat direction is $23$ years old (Renart, Song \& Wang 2003), so a corrective term is not our idea, and nothing here is a first report of the destroy-and-restore pattern. A local learning rule can \emph{produce} accurate ring-attractor tuning (Vafidis et al.\ 2022), so nothing we measure implies that learning cannot build a flat direction. And approximate continuous attractors arise in other task-trained recurrent networks by themselves, with a recent analytical model of the slow structure appearing during training (Dinc et al.\ 2025, preprint), where \S\ref{sec:cure} runs the opposite direction. \emph{Absent is the exchange rate: how much retention a given transverse curvature buys, where that law stops holding, and whether it survives the correction. We measure all three on trained models, in closed form, with a regime map --- floor, crossover --- that a first-order flow cannot exhibit.}

Closest to our object among this audience's own work is Xu et al.\ (2023), where a Lie-group representation is carried in the recurrent state of a grid-cell network and the compact, connected, abelian representation forces the torus topology over a continuous attractor. That is our geometry, in a network that path-integrates; what we add to it is a price list for time. Two further neighbours ask the same question in different currency: the optimal packing of attractor states in a representation, where symmetries of the environment's transition statistics imply symmetries of the optimal code (Vastola 2024), and memory modification approached through symmetry and geometry (D\"onmez 2024). Exact-versus-relaxed equivariance is this audience's own name for our designed-versus-emergent axis (van der Ouderaa \& van der Wilk 2023). Reading a landscape by its level sets and critical points is likewise native here (Akhtiamov \& Thomson 2023; Wang \& Ponce 2023, whose level sets are of tuning landscapes over \emph{stimuli}, not of a latent-state potential --- the two must not be conflated). A quotient coordinate has been used as an input encoding (Aslan, Platt \& Sheard 2023), where ours is a storage device.

A recent preprint on symmetry-protected Lyapunov neutral modes (arXiv:2605.03338), the equivariant-Lyapunov preprint below, proves that a $C^1$ field exactly equivariant under a Lie group has at least $\dim(G/\mathcal H)$ zero Lyapunov exponents tangent to the orbit; we use its diagnostics and its breaking-and-censoring protocol, and \S\ref{sec:headtohead} runs its estimator unchanged on our trained models. That result is the \emph{kinematics} of protection, silent on what sets the gap; here the gap is \emph{constitutive}, and Lyapunov spectra are blind to the write semantics --- transport, latch, noise accumulation --- that make a neutral direction a \emph{memory}. The theorem states sufficiency; equivariance is not necessary, the latch being a modulus of $V_\theta$. Constructive equivariant memory (Di Bernardo et al.\ 2025; Keller 2025; Iqbal, Keller, Song, Miyato \& Welling 2026) engineers \emph{which} symmetry a network expresses but does not price \emph{time}: we claim no novelty for choosing a symmetry group or coset, only the price list attached to a chosen structure. Cross-session representational drift (Driscoll et al.\ 2017; Aitken et al.\ 2022; Jude et al.\ 2023) is a different phenomenon from motion along a flat direction and we do not address it; the only motion we report is of the coset coordinate under our own dynamics. Our comparators are LSTM (Hochreiter \& Schmidhuber 1997), LEM (Rusch et al.\ 2022) and coRNN (Rusch \& Mishra 2021a); conformal symplecticity is McLachlan \& Perlmutter (2001), the leapfrog $h<2$ limit standard (Hairer, Lubich \& Wanner 2006), and wake--sleep (Hinton et al.\ 1995) with PCD (Tieleman 2008) the contrastive objective, whose landscape distortion is classical (Fischer \& Igel 2010; Nijkamp et al.\ 2020).

{\paragraph{Four claims we retire, so that a reviewer's first objections are answered in writing (elaborated, with their numbers, in Appendix~\ref{app:pos}).} \emph{(1)} Attention and state-space models \emph{do} have retention-guarantee analogues, and HiPPO-LegS (Gu et al.\ 2020) is the counterexample. \emph{(2)} ``Retrieval is a rollout, not a weighted sum'' is Kong, Brewer \& Lai's (2024) move, not our novelty. \emph{(3)} Continuity is no escape hatch from the NTM/DNC lineage (Graves et al.\ 2014, 2016; Csord\'as \& Schmidhuber 2019), which was already fully soft. \emph{(4)} This is no competitor to the transformer, and was never claimed to be. Our claim is correspondingly narrow: a physics-structured associative memory in which retention is a designed, per-item property --- permanent (symmetry-protected, $\mu^2\equiv0$ exactly) or of a finite half-life set by $\mu^2$ --- rather than an emergent consequence of a learned recurrence --- \emph{controllability}, which we evidence, not \emph{capability}, which we do not claim against transformers or state-space models. The nearest published neighbour to our write is Titans (Behrouz et al.\ 2025), test-time gradient descent with momentum on an associative-memory loss: the same family of discretized damped second-order dynamics, differing in that our damping and inertia are \emph{read out and priced}, not optimizer hyperparameters.\par}

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{The map, and the three bands.} One dissipative velocity-Verlet step with step $\varepsilon$ and damping $\gamma$ advances $(q,p)$ by a kick--drift--kick of $H=T(p)+V_\theta(q)$ followed by $p\mapsto(1-\gamma)p$. It is conformally symplectic --- $J^\top\Omega J=(1-\gamma)\Omega$, $\det J=(1-\gamma)^d$: phase-space volume contracts by a fixed factor per step, so the geometry is preserved and only the scale is spent. Memory at each normal mode reduces to one $2\times2$ block ($\mu^2=k/m$, $h=\varepsilon\mu$) with three bands: a latch ($\mu=0$, symmetry-protected) with frozen displacement $q_\infty=q_0+\varepsilon p_0/(M\gamma)$ and infinite half-life; an overdamped register ($0<\varepsilon\mu\lesssim\gamma/2$) at $n_{1/2}\approx2\gamma\ln2/[(2-\gamma)(\varepsilon\mu)^2]$; and an underdamped working memory ($\gamma/2\lesssim\varepsilon\mu<2$) at the curvature-independent envelope half-life $2\ln2/(-\ln(1-\gamma))$. The boundary is the exceptional point $h^*\approx\gamma/2$, the overdamped-to-underdamped crossover at which the block becomes defective. Relaxation onto the orbit is the fast normal flow; retention is the slow tangent flow. One difference from a classical neural integrator we do not smooth over: an integrator stores by \emph{not} damping, whereas at $\gamma=0$ our flat mode never freezes a write and any $\gamma>0$ gives an exact latch.

\paragraph{What the curvature can be, and what the unit stores.} At a critical point of $V_\theta$ the transverse curvature takes one of three values, and trained models here realize all three: \emph{exactly} zero along $\dim(G/\mathcal H)$ directions when the trained minimisers form a $G$-orbit (a Nambu--Goldstone mode --- the designed unit, and the exactly-flat-direction geometry); small but nonzero when an explicit tilt breaks the orbit, giving a slow manifold rather than a flat one (a pseudo-Goldstone mode --- the \emph{explicitly-broken cell}, which the analytic tilts of Appendix~\ref{app:gmor} instantiate and the self-broken MLP exhibits); and strictly positive with no orbit of minimisers at all when the minimiser is itself invariant, degenerate within each irrep by Schur's lemma (the contrastive-divergence--eroded vacuum of \S\ref{sec:cure}). Only the \emph{classical, tree-level} Goldstone theorem is used, a statement about a Hessian. No thermodynamic limit, no phase transition, no $\hbar$, and no loops, chiral logarithms, running low-energy constants or anomalies anywhere in this paper. What the unit stores is the \emph{coset coordinate}, the position along the flat direction; the map's $(\varepsilon,\gamma,T)$ fixes what a given $\mu^2$ buys, through the three bands above.

\paragraph{Trained models.} All checkpoints are the reference unit's Experiment-D configuration --- dim $4$, hidden $64$, learned-Newtonian kinetic term, $\mathrm{dt}=0.05$, unit circle at $256$ points, $150$ epochs (\S\ref{sec:cure} for why not the default $1000$) --- in two families: designed, an architecturally-$SO(2)$-invariant $V_\theta$ on $5$ seeds, and emergent, a generic MLP $V_\theta$ on the same data on $3$ seeds.

{
\emph{Fine print, next to the claims it qualifies.} \emph{(a)} The latch's infinite half-life is a $T=0$ statement about the deterministic damped map; at $T>0$ the coset coordinate diffuses with $D_\theta=\varepsilon T(2-\gamma)/(2F^2\gamma)$, $F^2=M_{\rm ch}r^{*2}$ --- a \emph{diffusive register} of finite computable lifetime, verified on the trained designed checkpoints, where raising friction \emph{lengthens} coset memory by $3.77\pm0.23\times$ for $\gamma:0.05\to0.2$ on $5/5$ seeds, and holding only under fluctuation--dissipation-consistent noise and a Newtonian kinetic mode, never under the reference implementation's legacy noise default. The checkpoints here are \texttt{newtonian\_learned}, so every finite-temperature number here is in scope. Nothing in \S\ref{sec:results} depends on it: all main-text retention results are $T=0$ deterministic rollouts.

\section{Results: the price list, its evidence, and its boundary}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{What a curvature buys, and where the law stops}\label{sec:pricelist}

A zero eigenvalue certifies that a direction is neutral. \emph{It does not say what a nearly-zero one costs.} \emph{We therefore tilt a trained potential by a known amount, read the resulting transverse curvature off its Hessian, and measure the half-life of a written value.} On the trained designed vacuum, whose flat mode has $\mu^2\le2.4\!\times\!10^{-15}$ and is machine-flat, the constitutive identity holds: the Hessian-derived spectral mass equals the measured one-step-Jacobian gap to $3.2\!\times\!10^{-10}$ over all $70$ tilt rows. The retention law follows, with the curvature ratio $\mu^2_{\mathrm{meas}}/\mu^2_{\mathrm{pred}}=1.000000\pm5\!\times\!10^{-12}$ at every tilt and seed across $4.5$ decades and a fitted overdamped slope of $-0.985$ ($n=35$; predicted $-1$). Past the crossover the half-life stops depending on curvature at all, saturating at the floor $27.03$ steps at $\gamma=0.05$. Two second-order signatures confirm that the crossover is a genuine exceptional --- defective --- point and not a fitting artifact: the retention metric bifurcates there, envelope half-life and first-crossing time agreeing to $0.01\%$ overdamped and splitting by $3.2\times$ at the deepest tilt, which is why every lifetime in this paper names its metric; and the oscillation frequency turns on with the predicted square root, $\varphi=0$ exactly below $h^*$ ($15/15$ rows) and $\varphi\propto\sqrt{h-h^*}$ above it at fitted slope $0.5165$ against the predicted $1/2$. The latch corner of the same block is the transport law $q_\infty=q_0+\varepsilon p_0/(M\gamma)$, which holds to $\le1\%$ over two decades of $\gamma$, the written angle then frozen to $\le1.2\!\times\!10^{-15}$ rad. \emph{These are trained checkpoints carrying analytic tilts: verification of the theory's exactness on a learned potential, not a discovery.} Appendix~\ref{app:gmor} records the battery in full, the probe's own roundoff floor, and the damping corollary that makes $n_{1/2}(\gamma)$ non-monotone.

\subsection{Evidence: a published lifetime law is the price list's overdamped face}\label{sec:headtohead}

The price list should predict what other instruments see. \emph{A published estimator gives the direct test, because it was fitted to a different object.} \emph{We therefore run it unchanged on our trained models and read where it holds.} The equivariant-Lyapunov preprint (arXiv:2605.03338) shows that a ``pseudo-gap'' predicts a finite memory lifetime through a single-exponential estimator. Running its exact code-level protocol --- phase $0.35$ rad, threshold $0.2$ rad, its censoring, cap $15000$ steps --- unchanged on the $5$ trained models across $14$ breaking magnitudes gives measured/predicted $1.012\pm0.000\to1.029\pm0.001$ overdamped against its reported median $1.013$, $2.202\pm0.155$ at the exceptional point, and $0.309\pm0.012$ deep underdamped. That law is the overdamped face of our price list: there the two coincide, $\mathrm{corr}(\log\mathrm{pred},\log\mathrm{meas})=0.9987$ overdamped-only, and its predictor fails only past the crossover --- by $\approx3.2\times$ at the deepest trained-model breaking we test, $\delta=4$, our sweep going no deeper underdamped --- in the calculable ballistic direction, with the $2.2\times$ exceptional-point delay spike as the third signature. On the \emph{exact analytic map} the same mechanism continues to $\approx5\times$ further underdamped (theory note, check (k), $\gamma=0.1$); the $\approx3.2\times$ is the trained-model number, the $\approx5\times$ is exact-map only, and we never quote it as evidence about a trained network. This is containment, not conflict: below $h^*$ we recover its reported number, and above it the regime structure --- floor, ringing, exceptional point --- is what a first-order flow cannot exhibit and what a \emph{constitutive} curvature adds. It also does not rest on substituting the exact Jacobian gap for its predictor: the result reproduces on the preprint's own finite-horizon estimator (Appendix~\ref{app:pos}). \emph{Evidence: a recently posted ML lifetime law, run on trained checkpoints.}

\subsection{The honest negative: where the price list does not extend}\label{sec:boundary}

Symmetric training data might be expected to induce a flat direction on its own. \emph{On this architecture class it does not.} A generic MLP potential trained on that data develops \emph{no} near-flat direction: softest emergent $\mu^2=5.1/5.9/5.4\!\times\!10^{-2}$ on $3$ seeds against the designed flat mode $\le2.4\!\times\!10^{-15}$, a gap of $13$--$14$ orders; the preprint's own attribution instrument separates the architectures by $15$ orders. This is a measurement on our architecture class and training recipe, not a general statement that learning cannot produce a tuned flat direction --- a local learning rule that does produce one is published (Vafidis et al.\ 2022). Operationally that arm stores nothing on a \emph{continuum}: $\approx1$--$1.6$ bits on $2$--$3$ washboard minima, a written $\delta$ relaxing completely, though the damping-optimum retention law itself still generalizes to it. The continuous coset register is therefore designed-only, and we mark it as the one face of the price list that does not transfer.

\paragraph{The same boundary against learned baselines.} Gated and oscillatory recurrent networks fit this task well. \emph{Fitting it is not holding it.} \emph{We therefore write a value into each and let it run autonomously.} On the preprint's $S^1$ path-integration family the baselines are trained with a learning-rate sweep, best RMSE kept, and are not strawmen: LSTM and LEM reach train-horizon RMSE $0.18$/$0.23$ rad. Yet under its \emph{autonomous} protocol the structural triad of latch, $\mu^{-2}$ law and bounded motion of the stored coordinate is qualitatively absent in them: coRNN/LEM/LSTM forget the stored analog phase in $\approx5.6/56/69$ map-steps, the stored coordinate randomizes past $1.2$ rad, and they are \emph{perturbation-fragile}, a $0.1$ hidden kick collapsing LSTM $69\to2$ and LEM $56\to5$, while the generically-trained unit holds $\approx263$ map-steps with bounded motion of that coordinate ($\approx0.35$ rad, price-list law to $+12$--$15\%$) and the designed unit never moves ($5/5$ seeds). \emph{Evidence.}

\paragraph{Honest gap.} The load-bearing claim is the qualitative triad, architecture- and compute-independent. The raw $263$-versus-$69$ map-step ratio does not survive compute normalization, and we retire it as a compute claim: per step the Verlet update costs $\approx6.2\times$ an LSTM cell and $\approx3.1\times$ a LEM cell in wall time, $\approx14$--$15\times$ FLOPs, not width-matched, so per unit of retention the unit spends $\approx23.5\times$/$\approx14.6\times$ more wall time than the baselines it out-holds (Appendix~\ref{app:compute}). Lacking native velocity ingestion, it cannot enter the preprint's input-driven task-RMSE axis at all, so no task-RMSE was fabricated.

\subsection{The price list survives the training-time correction}\label{sec:cure}

A price list read off a potential is worth little if the training objective quietly rewrites the potential. \emph{Ours does exactly that.} Wake--sleep contrastive divergence drives the order parameter $r^*$ to zero, inverting the designed vacuum in $8/8$ runs at the default $1000$ epochs: the minimiser becomes invariant, with the degenerate $\mu^2$ pair predicted in \S\ref{sec:setup}, on a horizon set by sleep-update \emph{frequency} racing the wake clamp, and specific to flat or degenerate vacua. This is an instance of what the attractor literature calls the fine-tuning problem, the perturbation here being the training objective itself. {\emph{Scope clause, travelling with that finding:} our sweep varies chain length only over \texttt{sleep\_steps}$\in\{50,500\}$ at otherwise fixed sampler settings and does not resolve where either sits relative to this model's mixing time, so the finding is \emph{frequency-decisive across the two chain lengths we run}, and is not a claim that chain length is irrelevant to contrastive-divergence learning in general.\par} A $V(\text{data})$ energy anchor holds the condensate up --- $\lambda=100$: $5/5$ seeds, $r^*=0.911\pm0.016$, at the cost of weaker noise rejection and $\approx35\times$ higher wake MSE. \emph{That a corrective term can keep a flat direction alive is not new} (Renart, Song \& Wang 2003). What we add is the third part of the price list: every headline law of it still holds under the correction, at $\approx20\times$ the erosion horizon (Appendix~\ref{app:anchor}, Figure~\ref{fig:sf3}) --- $3000$ anchored epochs, $3$ seeds, curvature ratio exact to $1.5\!\times\!10^{-12}$ over $4.6$ decades, slope $-0.956$, the same floor, and the exceptional-point onset bit-identical at $0.5165$. The architecture \emph{breaks} the symmetry, the objective \emph{restores} it, the anchor \emph{keeps it broken}.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

{
\emph{Scope box --- limitations and scope choices, stated so no reader has to infer them.} All results are laptop-CPU, dim $4$, the $S^1$ testbed, $\le5$ seeds; the solvable core is quadratic and trained statements are local at a critical point of $V_\theta$, with anharmonic deviations measured as a zero-kick retention bias of $+5$ to $+29\%$, kick-independent for $2/3$ emergent seeds and amplitude-dependent for the softest-$\mu^2$ seed. The head-to-head is on \emph{autonomous retention} in map-application units, and the unit does not enter the input-driven task-RMSE axis. BIBO boundedness is evidenced within the coercive-potential / compact-sublevel-set scope and concerns the autonomous, designed setting measured here --- nothing here claims that symplectic structure buys off-distribution stability in a \emph{trained, input-driven} setting, which we do not test and where a published symplectic architecture already holds the ground with a gradient bound --- and friction never stabilizes a saddle (Appendix~\ref{app:neg}). The symmetry framing is tree-level only. The finite-$T$ diffusion law holds on both the designed channel (verification) and the emergent arm (evidence, $3$ seeds, above $T^\star\approx3\!\times\!10^{-3}$) under FDT-consistent noise in a Newtonian kinetic mode; the one face that does not generalize is the continuous latch register, which is designed-only. \emph{(i) Scale is out of scope by choice:} every number here is dim $4$, hidden $64$, $\le5$ seeds, single-unit, laptop-CPU, and we report no result at larger dimension, wider layers, more units or accelerator scale. \emph{(ii) No external benchmark is won on its own headline metric anywhere in this paper}; the comparisons are diagnostic retention protocols on a synthetic $S^1$ family and matched-parameter ablations, not leaderboard results --- and \S\ref{sec:boundary}'s honest gap is part of the claim, not a caveat to it. \emph{(iii) The substrate scope, once, in our own voice: these laws govern the store; end-to-end performance additionally depends on the encoder, measured separately, with its parameter and state-byte budget ledgered.}
\par}

A price list changes what a flat direction is for. Read this way a trained memory has latches --- exact flat directions, the moduli of $V_\theta$ --- and registers, nearly-flat modes of a chosen half-life, fenced as designed-geometry in Appendix~\ref{app:neg}; kinetic isotropy is then the price of an equivariant write \emph{current}, not of the register. Named as directions, not results: the $\mu^{-2}$ law, crossover and floor re-measured at latent dimension $\gtrsim64$ and $N\ge8$ interacting units, where mode mixing can break the single-block reduction that makes the price list exact here; the head-to-head on a wider trained-model family; the anchored recipe at longer training and larger width; the equivariant-control wrapper that would put the unit on the supervised task axis; a learned-store re-test of the explicitly-broken cell respecting that fence; the map from $(\mu,\gamma,T)$ to lifetime whose $T=0$ face is \S\ref{sec:results}; and the price list re-derived beyond abelian $SO(2)$, for a torus $T^2$ or the non-abelian $SO(3)\to SO(2)$ coset --- a direction, on which this paper offers no evidence.

\textbf{A reporting caution travels with the isotropization row.} The closed-form charge-oscillation amplitude is an \textbf{on-vacuum-orbit} statement; \textbf{off the orbit only boundedness holds}. The amplitude-to-split ratio is moreover \textbf{not} constant, as the exact envelope requires, since it is linear only to leading order --- so no proportionality constant should be quoted as a law, and we report amplitudes and boundedness, never a ``drift rate''. The design rule attached to mass-tying is correspondingly about the \emph{current}, not the register: \textbf{an anisotropic channel still latches with infinite half-life}; tie the masses for an angle-independent write current, and at $\dim(G/\mathcal H)\ge2$ a degenerate pseudo-Goldstone multiplet, \textbf{not} to save the register.

\textbf{Two reading rules travel with the learned-store row.} \emph{(a)} The breaking coefficient of that battery is an \textbf{explicit-symmetry-breaking magnitude}; it is \textbf{not} this paper's integrator step $\varepsilon$, and the two must never be conflated. \emph{(b)} No sentence in this paper may be read as claiming that a tilt magnitude is a lifetime dial on a learned store, or that a pseudo-Goldstone lifetime can be extended without bound by shrinking the breaking: on a store with a coercive confinement $\alpha\lVert q\rVert^2$ the ceiling is $\Gamma/2\alpha$, and lowering $\alpha$ breaks the write. This paper's own tilt results (Appendix~\ref{app:gmor}) are analytic tilts on \textbf{designed} architecturally-invariant potentials, where the construction is exact; the learned-store scope is exactly what this negative removes. That battery is a \emph{different architecture} from the trained units of this paper and shares none of their training configuration, which is why it is quoted as a scope fence and never as a measurement of the units here. What survives it is the \emph{geometry}: the identity $\lambda_{\min}=\epsilon$ holds in the single-atom geometry in which it was derived, unit-tested to $5\%$, and the registered damped-mode floor $\tau=2/\Gamma$ is confirmed, $5.0$ predicted against $4.23$--$6.58$ measured time units.

\textbf{The sampler row's scope, stated precisely, because the qualifier is load-bearing.} The coded damping substep is an additive Gaussian momentum kick of fixed covariance, so the chain's invariant momentum marginal is necessarily Gaussian-smoothed; for a \emph{relativistic} kinetic term the Gibbs momentum marginal is Maxwell--J\"uttner, which is not of that form (\emph{theory note}, Prop-9$'$ --- a characteristic-function argument needing neither $V=0$ nor linear damping). \textbf{The failure is in the \emph{sampler}, not in the \emph{thermodynamics}}: the configurational equilibrium $\pi_q\propto e^{-V_\theta/T}$ is relativity-insensitive, since the momentum integral factorizes out, so a relativistic unit's equilibrium distribution is perfectly well-defined and the chain simply fails to sample it; a one-line thermostat carrying a randomized, momentum-dependent inertia equal to the relativistic mass repairs it. We therefore never assert that a relativistic unit ``has no equilibrium''. \textbf{This touches no result in this paper:} the trained units here are \texttt{newtonian\_learned} throughout, so $\sigma^\star$ is exact for every configuration we run --- the qualifier is a \emph{scope clause} on a class-level theorem, not a correction to a measurement.


\section{Positioning: the four retirements in full, and the head-to-head on the preprint's own estimator}\label{app:pos}

\emph{The four retirements of \S\ref{sec:related}, elaborated so that every number behind them is on the record.}

\textbf{(1) Attention and state-space models have retention-guarantee analogues.} HiPPO-LegS (Gu et al.\ 2020) has an $O(tL/\sqrt N)$ approximation bound, a polynomial $\Theta(1/t)$ rather than exponential gradient decay, and \emph{exact} timescale equivariance with no discretization step to tune --- exact where ours is a discrete map at fixed $\varepsilon$ with $h=\varepsilon\mu<2$ a hard stability limit --- and we have no measurement saying a learned curvature spectrum is preferable to it. Tunable permanence is likewise occupied (EDEN, Karuvally et al.\ 2025); UnICORNN (Rusch \& Mishra 2021b) is Hamiltonian, structure-preserving and \emph{has} the gradient-bound theorem we lack; and the modern Hopfield update \emph{coincides with} attention for a single head with identity projections and $\beta=1/\sqrt D$ (Ramsauer et al.\ 2021).

\textbf{(2) ``Retrieval is a rollout, not a weighted sum'' is not our novelty.} The move is Kong, Brewer \& Lai's (2024), at capacity $N_c\propto K^{1.08\pm0.01}$ for their one-hot and binary index codings and $1.17\pm0.02$ for their 2D coding. Our differences are structural: neither symplectic, Hamiltonian nor mass-structured there, and the address enters through an index channel rather than as the initial condition of the state.

\textbf{(3) Continuity is not an escape hatch from the NTM/DNC lineage.} Both were already fully soft (Graves et al.\ 2014, 2016), so the precedent transfers as a warning. Of the three DNC failure modes Csord\'as \& Schmidhuber (2019) diagnose, key/value entanglement, which they rank first, is architectural in a memory whose address $q_0$ is both the key and the initial condition of the value; de-allocation aliasing maps onto any position-gated forgetting; chained-read degradation is the third. None are measured here.

\textbf{(4) Not a competitor to the transformer.} Never claimed, and indefensible at this scale: $\approx1$--$1.6$ bits per emergent register, no win over a trivial baseline on any external benchmark, and formal limits on fixed-size latent state for copying and retrieval (Jelassi et al.\ 2024) that conservation does not evade --- it prevents \emph{decay}, not \emph{capacity}.

\textbf{The head-to-head reproduced on the preprint's own instrument.} \S\ref{sec:headtohead}'s result does not depend on substituting the exact Jacobian gap for the preprint's predictor. With the preprint's own finite-horizon estimator $\hat\lambda(T{=}128)$ the result holds on that instrument: $\mathrm{corr}=0.9995$ overdamped, meas/pred $0.86$--$1.03$, and $0.30$ at $\delta=4$ against the exact-gap $0.31$.

\textbf{F.2 The damping corollary} --- a supporting result, deliberately not a headline. Below $\gamma^*$ the mode is underdamped at the floor $2\ln2/(-\ln(1-\gamma))$, which \emph{shortens} as $\gamma$ grows; above it the mode is overdamped at $n_{1/2}\approx2\gamma\ln2/[(2-\gamma)(\varepsilon\mu)^2]$, which \emph{lengthens}; the exceptional point is where the branches meet.
