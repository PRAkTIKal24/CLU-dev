## Open questions / follow-ups / risks

1. ⭐ **The `d` decision is now a Hub call, not a measurement.** Geometry favours **16**; the store is
   **inert** there. `d = 12` digs but only 2/6 designed writes produced a usable well. **If the spine
   needs healthy wells more than it needs 6 % of σ_q/spacing, run it at `d = 8`.** My recommendation
   stands at **12**, with `8` as the conservative fallback; either way the label travels.
2. ⭐ **Should the spine use `randconv` instead of `simclr`?** It is free (2.9 s vs 41 min/seed), has
   the same address geometry, and the **only** thing it loses is ACC — which this pass does not score.
   That would cut the spine's φ cost by ~2 h/3 seeds. ⛔ It would also change what "strong φ" means in
   the spine's provenance sentence, so it is the Hub's call, not mine.
3. ⚠ **The depth probe writes 6 designed sites, not a stream.** It answers "does the store dig at this
   `d`?" and nothing else; it is **not** a census and must not be quoted as one.
4. ⚠ **`n = 16` is small.** SEs on 3 seeds at `n = 16` are what they are; the arm ordering is stable
   across `n ∈ {16, 40, 200}`, which is the robustness evidence I have, but the *absolute* σ_q/spacing
   values move ~35 % between `n = 16` and `n = 200` and must always be quoted with their `n`.
5. ⚠ **`pca_whiten` is implemented and unrun.** It is the one map form that could plausibly move
   σ_q/spacing further (it equalises per-axis variance), and §4 warns that axis rescales are exactly
   the class of move that can look like information gain. Someone should either run it *with* that
   caveat or delete it.
