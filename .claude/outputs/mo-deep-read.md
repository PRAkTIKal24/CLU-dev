## 1. Precise reconstruction of Mo's framework

### 1.1 Theorem 1 — exact setting and statement

Setting: finite-dimensional smooth manifold $M$, **continuous-time autonomous $C^1$ vector field** $\dot x = f(x)$ with complete flow $\phi_t$; Lie group $G$ acting smoothly; equivariance $f(g\cdot x) = D(g)_x f(x)$ (their Eq. 1) $\Rightarrow \phi_t(g\cdot x)=g\cdot\phi_t(x)$. Group tangent bundle $E^G_x = T_x(G\cdot x) = \{\xi_M(x):\xi\in\mathfrak g\}$ (Eq. 2).

**Assumption 1 (persistent nondegenerate orbit bundle):** compact invariant $K$ with invariant measure $\mu$ (Oseledets), constant stabilizer type $H$ on $K$, $\operatorname{rank}E^G_x=q=\dim(G/H)$, and the infinitesimal action map $A_x:\mathfrak g\to T_xM$ has nonzero singular values **uniformly bounded above and below** on $K$.

**Theorem 1:** $E^G$ is invariant under the tangent cocycle $D\phi_t$, and wherever Lyapunov limits exist ($\mu$-a.e.), the restricted cocycle $D\phi_t|_{E^G}$ has all $q$ exponents $=0$; hence the full spectrum contains **at least** $q$ zeros counted with multiplicity.

**Proof mechanism (matters for the audit):** differentiate the flow identity to get $D\phi_t(x)\,\xi_M(x)=\xi_M(\phi_t(x))$; uniform nondegeneracy gives two-sided bounds $cC^{-1}\|v\| \le \|D\phi_t(x)v\| \le Cc^{-1}\|v\|$ for $v\in E^G_x$, **uniform in $t$** — i.e. the theorem actually certifies *uniform Lyapunov stability* of the restricted cocycle and extracts the zero-exponent consequence. Everything is derived from the cocycle identity + orbit geometry; **no structure of $f$ beyond equivariance is used**. That is its strength (generality) and — for us — its boundary (it can never output constitutive constants; §2a).

"Lyapunov neutral" therefore quantifies exactly: **asymptotic exponential growth rate zero of group-tangent perturbations**, with uniform-in-time norm bounds under the stated assumptions. Scope guards they state themselves: (i) "at least" — extra zeros can come from *time translation, additional symmetries, conservation laws, or finite-time numerics* (note: **conservation laws appear once in the whole paper, as a confound**, not a mechanism); (ii) the theorem is **autonomous-flow only** — *"input-driven dynamics require separate equivariant-control assumptions"* (§4); the trained task results are labeled "consequence evidence," not theorem evidence; (iii) finite circulant grids are excluded (exact $C_N$ but not $S^1$ symmetry); (iv) approximate equivariance = unprotected unless further bounds are established.

**Model class used everywhere:** first-order flows. Theorem-level systems (their Table A2): $S^1$, $T^q$, $SO(n)/SO(n-1)$, $U(m)/U(m-1)$, product groups, one coupled irrep "RNN-style branch" ($\rho_\theta(z,w,h)=(e^{i\theta}z, e^{2i\theta}w, h)$) — all **fixed-point orbit attractors ($f=0$ on the orbit)** plus one relative-equilibrium control. The learned cell is $dz/dt = a(I,h,u)\,z + b(I,h,u)\,Jz$, $dh/dt=g(I,h,u)$, invariant feature $I=\|z\|^2$ — **first order in the state; there is no momentum, no inertia, no second-order structure anywhere in the paper** [verified by full-text term counts + reading every system definition].

### 1.2 The pseudo-gap — what it actually is

**No closed-form definition appears in the paper.** It is operational, and the code pins it down (`exp31...py`, `compute_diagnostics`):

- Break the symmetry explicitly with magnitude $\epsilon$ (theorem-level: perturbation matrices, families `weak_axis`, `unit_axis`, `rotated_strong`, + a random anisotropic ensemble; learned: `clone_with_epsilon(eps)` adds an $\epsilon$-parameterized non-equivariant term to the trained cell; $\epsilon\in\{10^{-4},3\times10^{-4},10^{-3},3\times10^{-3},10^{-2},3\times10^{-2},6\times10^{-2}\}$).
- Measure the **direct group-tangent exponent** $\widehat\lambda_\xi(T)=\frac1T\log\frac{\|D\phi_T(x)\xi_M(x)\|}{\|\xi_M(x)\|}$ (their Eq. 5) under the **zero-input autonomous restriction**, from initial phase $\phi_0=0.35$, horizon 128 steps, $dt=0.1$.
- **pseudo-gap $:= \max(0, -\widehat\lambda)$** — i.e. the (nonnegative) decay rate that the formerly-protected direction acquires. It is a **Lyapunov-spectrum object of a dissipative/contractive flow**, full stop. The scout's characterization is confirmed.
- **Lifetime, measured:** first time the stored phase has drifted by threshold $0.2$ rad from its initial value ($|\phi(t)-\phi_0|\ge 0.2$), max $t=1500$ ($1.5\times10^4$ steps); runs that never cross are **censored** and excluded from the correlation (reported as "uncensored fraction").
- **Lifetime, predicted:** $t_{\rm pred} = -\ln\!\big(1 - \mathrm{th}/|\phi_0|\big)\big/\mathrm{gap}$ — the paper calls this "the local exponential memory-loss formula" (code comment) — i.e. **pure single-exponential relaxation toward the pinning point is assumed**: $\phi(t)=\phi_0 e^{-\mathrm{gap}\cdot t}$, solve for the threshold crossing.
- **Reported numbers, exact scope:** hand-constructed breaking families — pseudo-gap↔log-lifetime correlation $0.9999999886$, uncensored fraction $1.0$, median measured/predicted ratio $\approx 1.013$ (Fig. 4). Learned cell, post-training breaking — correlation $0.9991$ over uncensored rows, uncensored fraction $0.857$ (=6/7; the $\epsilon=10^{-4}$ row plausibly censored) (Fig. A7, Table A3). Fig. 4C: "measured symmetry-direction exponents track perturbative predictions" — gap linear in $\epsilon$ at leading order.

**Interpretation [proven by their own formula]:** since $t_{\rm pred}\propto 1/\mathrm{gap}$ *by definition*, the corr ≈ 1 result establishes that **their tested systems are single-regime exponential relaxers** — the correlation is a (well-executed) consistency check of first-order dynamics, not a nontrivial constitutive law. This is the pivot of the whole audit: the substance of a "gap predicts lifetime" claim lives entirely in **what sets the gap**, and there their content is "linear in $\epsilon$, perturbatively" for first-order broken orbit-attractors.
