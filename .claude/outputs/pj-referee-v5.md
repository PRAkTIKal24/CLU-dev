## A. VERDICT (simulated PALM review)

**Score: 3/10 — REJECT.** (Borderline-reject at the most generous reading; a *weak accept* is reachable, and cheaply, because every fix is a **restore** from the clean base rather than a new experiment.)

**Meta-review, in the voice of the composite reviewer.** This paper works in ground the CFP names four times ("memory update and deletion tests", "right-to-be-forgotten mechanisms", forgetting, deletion) and almost nobody occupies, and its underlying results — a predicted retention optimum at γ_crit = 2εμ that survives a second instrument, a friction hole that measurably confines a stored coordinate, and a store whose post-deletion state is bit-identical to one that never held the item — are exactly the mechanisms this room says it wants and does not have. **I want to accept this work. I cannot accept this artifact.** Three things stop me, and none is about the science. First, the paper has **four references, three of which it never cites**, and a Related Work section that names MemGPT, Mem0, a `0.995` decay factor and the reconstructibility of soft-deleted vectors in graph databases **without a single citation** — those are specific results from specific papers, and one of them (Titans' learned forget gate) is the *nearest published neighbour* to this paper's central claim and is authored by an invited speaker at this workshop. Second, the paper's own claims are not bounded by their own evidence: it says the retention law "generalizes seamlessly to emergent units" on p.3 and that continuous cosets "do not natively emerge during generalized MLP training" on p.7; it says deletion leaves "no measurable trace" on p.2 and that the store "ceases answering queries prior to halting internal data leakage" on p.4; it says the V-curve is robust "independently of the chosen measurement instrument" on p.3 and prints a table on p.6 in which two of four instruments return an argmin of 0.086–0.30 against the claimed 0.90. Third — and this is the one I would not waive — the Limitations section says **"Certified removal is proven"** while the paper never states a certificate, never defines an (ε,δ) condition, and never cites the certified-removal literature. Individually these are fixable in an afternoon. Together they read as a paper written faster than it was checked, and I cannot certify to the AC that its claims are bounded by its evidence.

**What I would say to the authors:** the honest version of this paper already exists inside it. Put the seed counts, the read tolerance Δ, the `fdt`+Newtonian precondition, the designed-only rider on the field-vs-scalar contrast, the flat-table trivial substitute, the three deletion conditions, the recency exclusion, the Blelloch–Golovin priority disclaimer, and ~25 citations back — and this is a paper I argue for.

**Track fit: it reads as a SHORT paper, and that is the problem.** Measured on my build: main text occupies **4 pages full + 3.5 % of a fifth** (`pdftotext -bbox`, block 72–720 pt; p.4 last line yMax 722.2, p.5 last line yMax 95.0) = **4.04 pp**, total 7 pp (refs p.6, appendices A–E pp.6–7, one figure, four tables). It is ~2 lines over "up to 4 pages" and would be compliant after a trivial trim. But **the register does not match the length**: it opens with a three-contribution list, an operational-assumptions list and a nomenclature paragraph — full-paper furniture — and then spends its remaining budget asserting three contributions it has no room to defend. **My recommendation is the 9-pp full track.** The clean base's 6 pp is the paper's honest size; the short track was bought by deleting the evidence apparatus, and I would rather referee a compact 6-pp full-track paper than a 4-pp paper whose caveats are missing. The stated counter-risk (full-track referees demand real data) is real but *smaller* than the risk this artifact runs today: a full-track reviewer asks for an experiment you can name as future work; this artifact invites a reviewer to doubt the claims you already have.
