## The review (as I would submit it on OpenReview)

### 1. Summary
The paper proposes treating forgetting in a memory store as an intrinsic dynamical property rather than a bookkeeping policy. The store is a damped symplectic integrator of a learned Hamiltonian; retention of a stored direction is governed by three physical parameters (spectral mass μ, damping γ, temperature T). Three results are claimed: (1) retention half-life is non-monotone in friction with a computable optimum γ_crit = 2εμ, with the law collapsing designed and learned-MLP units onto one curve across ~11 orders of magnitude in μ²; (2) retention is scopeable — at T = 0 a designed coset latches indefinitely under any friction, while at T > 0 only temperature erases, so a localized position-gated friction hole acts as a "memory vault" (107.77 ± 4.78× retention vs. 13.28 ± 0.12× for uniform scalar friction); (3) under canonical (history-independent) placement in the style of Blelloch–Golovin, store-level deletion is exact: the post-delete store is byte-identical to one that never held the item. All experiments are on a dim-4, hidden-64, CPU-scale testbed with 5 designed + 3 emergent seeds and a small (capacities 8–64) datastore; the authors state this explicitly.

### 2. Strengths
- **S1 — A genuinely different lens on retention, matched to the CFP.** Deployed memory systems (MemGPT, Mem0, Zep, TTLs, learned forget gates) enforce retention by rules; this paper derives half-lives, read tolerances, and an erasure/preservation dichotomy (∂n₁/₂/∂γ > 0 vs. ∂n₁/₂/∂T < 0, Table 1) from closed forms (App. F) that the measurements then reproduce to ~1% (Fig. 3c: 1.0068 ± 0.0219). The claim "friction preserves, temperature erases" is a crisp, falsifiable, and to my knowledge novel statement about a memory substrate, and the CFP explicitly welcomes theory papers.
- **S2 — Unusually honest scoping and adversarial self-testing.** The Scope paragraph and §3 pre-empt the obvious attacks (toy scale, designed-symmetry precondition, "no certified (ε,δ) unlearning," recency eviction excluded, no task-level results). Appendices show pre-registered predictions with stated falsifiers, including one that *fired* (App. D, ℓ375–379) and one comparison declared *void* (Table 5 caption) rather than quietly dropped. Cross-instrument verification (one-step Jacobian vs. nonlinear rollout, App. C, Fig. 10) and a laundering control (scalar-γ vs. field, Fig. 2b, Table 5) are exactly what a skeptical reviewer would demand.
- **S3 — The deletion result is correctly situated in the data-structures literature.** App. E states plainly what is old (Snyder 1977 → Blelloch–Golovin 2007 placement, delete-time fix-up) and what is claimed new (the composition with a decaying amplitude that commutes with deletion, F.8), and prices it (O(n) rebuild, history-independence cost per Buchbinder–Petrank). This is more care than most "we delete memories" papers in this space take.

### 3. Weaknesses (ranked)
- **W1 (major) — No evidence the room's problem is advanced, even at toy scale.** Every result characterizes the physics of a store the community does not use; there is no task, no retrieval benchmark, and — more importantly for a 4-page short — no head-to-head against the mechanisms the paper positions against (a decay factor, a TTL, a learned forget gate) on *any* memory objective at matched capacity/compute. §3 concedes "No Task-Level Results," but the introduction's motivating complaint (one cannot read a half-life off an LSTM) is never converted into a demonstration that being able to read one off buys anything. The single quantitative contact with a deployed primitive is adversarial and *negative for the paper's framing* (the TTL flag leaks within 0.017 AUC of physical decay, ℓ145–147). A workshop short is allowed to be narrow, but it should contain at least one panel where the (μ, γ, T) dial does something a TTL cannot.
- **W2 (major) — The strongest claims hold only on the designed architecture, and the paper's own experiments show the learned one lacks the substrate.** Fig. 8 is titled by the authors: "The emergent net has NO continuous coset register" (capacity ≈ 1–1.6 bits); the T = 0 latch "requires precise continuous coset geometries... explicitly designed in" (ℓ156–157); the emergent flat-mode floor misses by ~10⁻³ (Fig. 7b). What transfers to learned units is the V-curve exponents, the diffusion law above T* (App. D), and the refrigerator law (Table 4) — real, but the headline abilities (indefinite latch, byte-exact deletion, the quoted 107.77× vault) live on the hand-built arm. The emergent vault contrast arm *triggered its pre-registered falsifier* (23.39 ± 10.06 vs. bounds [6.5, 9.5], ℓ375–379); this caveat appears only on p. 14 and is not reflected where the vault is sold in §2.2 or the abstract.
- **W3 (major) — The vault headline number rests on an estimator choice a reviewer cannot adjudicate.** The raw first-passage measurement is 86.97 ± 2.94×; the quoted 107.77 ± 4.78× comes from the D̂_θ estimator after a boundary-layer-bias argument (ℓ120–123, Fig. 2c). The bias direction conveniently favors the larger number, the justification (ℓ_θ/Δ = 0.079) is one clause, and no bias-corrected first-passage reading is given. Quoting the model-based estimator over the direct measurement needs more than a caption.
- **W4 (major) — §2.3's "Lifetime Trilemma" is asserted, not established.** "A system may only optimize two" of {exact value fidelity, amplitude-independent address hold, amplitude-independent read latency} (ℓ141–143) is stated as a strict law; its three terms are never defined in the main text, and App. E supplies parameters (r = −0.85 with a_i², R₅₀ contraction) — i.e., one system tracing a trade-off — not a proof or an impossibility argument. Either call it an empirical trade-off or prove it.
- **W5 (moderate) — The privacy/MI treatment will not satisfy the MI people in the room.** Table 6's adversary models ("Query-level membership depth, fully exact," σ_obs = 0.1) are nonstandard and undefined in main text; there is no threat-model statement, no adversary capability/knowledge specification, no standard MI methodology or baselines. And AUC 0.983 vs. 1.000 means the adversary still essentially wins; the main text's framing ("stops answering external queries significantly before the actual structural data stops leaking") spins a negative result. Honest data, misleading rhetoric.
- **W6 (moderate) — The "11 orders of magnitude" collapse claim is undercut by the paper's own fine print.** The low endpoint of μ² ∈ [1.7×10⁻¹², 7×10⁻²] is "the ring-profile probe's resolution... machine zero rather than a spectral mass" (ℓ94–97). Eleven orders where the bottom of the range is an instrument floor rather than a measured mass is really "designed modes at μ² ~ 0.7–1.3, emergent modes at ~10⁻², plus one point pinned at the floor." The abstract's "approximately 11 orders of magnitude" should not survive its own footnote.
- **W7 (moderate) — Deletion guarantee excludes the deployed common case.** Recency-based eviction — the dominant policy in real agent memories — is excluded because it breaks history-independence (ℓ161–163, F.8). The exclusion is principled and honestly stated, but it means the exact-deletion result applies to a store class that deployed systems would have to be redesigned into; the paper should say what fraction of the motivating systems (Zep, Mem0, MemGPT) could adopt canonical placement at all.
- **W8 (minor) — Reproducibility floor.** No code or anonymous artifact link. Configuration is given as implementation flags (`langevin_noise="fdt"`, "Newtonian kinetic mode") of a codebase the reviewer cannot see; the actual update step appears only in App. F. Every empirical number is take-on-trust (see §6 below).

### 4. Questions to the authors
1. Can you show one experiment — however small — where tuning (μ, γ, T) achieves a retention/erasure profile that a TTL or decay-factor store at matched bytes cannot? This is the missing bridge to this workshop.
2. Why is the D̂_θ-based 107.77× "the quoted number" rather than the directly measured first-passage 86.97×? Can you show a boundary-layer-corrected first-passage estimate agreeing with 107.77?
3. The emergent contrast-arm falsifier fired (App. D: 23.39 vs. [6.5, 9.5]). What exactly does that falsify, and why does the main-text vault claim not carry this caveat?
4. Is the Lifetime Trilemma a theorem? If so, state and prove it; if not, what licenses "may only optimize two"?
5. In Table 6, define the adversary precisely. Against a standard query-level MI attack, what does 0.983 AUC buy anyone? What claim about privacy do you actually make?
6. Abstract says "3-dimensional datastore"; the Scope says "dimension of 4"; App. E measures "at n = 4" then "n ∈ {8,16,40,64}." Reconcile the dimensions/capacities.
7. Given Fig. 8 (learned units hold ≈ 1–1.6 bits), what is the path by which an *emergent* unit ever supports the latch/vault/deletion trio — is the designed-symmetry precondition removable in principle, or is this framework ultimately a specification for hand-built stores?
8. What is per-operation cost (read/write/delete wall-clock) vs. a flat vector store at the same capacity? App. E concedes O(n) rebuild per delete; is anything else competitive?

### 5. Scores (unhedged)
- **Overall recommendation: borderline.** For a non-archival, theory-welcoming workshop: the formal core is real, self-critical, and relevant to retention/deletion/consolidation themes; but the paper never touches the room's objects of study even at toy scale (W1), its strongest claims are confined to a hand-designed substrate that its own Fig. 8 shows learned units lack (W2), and the main text appears over the 4-page budget (§9). Fix the length, move the fired-falsifier caveat and estimator justification into the main text, and demonstrate one dial-vs-TTL contrast, and this becomes a weak accept I would defend.
- **Confidence: 4.** I checked the closed forms in App. F (F.1 Jacobian, F.5 FDT scale σ* = √(M_i T γ(2−γ)), F.3 branch asymptotics, F.6 diffusion law) and they are internally consistent; I cannot verify any measurement. A code/artifact link would raise this to 5.
- **Soundness: 3/4.** Derivations coherent and cross-instrumented; docked for the estimator-selection issue (W3), the asserted trilemma (W4), and the abstract-level overclaim on the 11-order collapse (W6).
- **Presentation: 2/4.** Dense private jargon, an illegible headline Figure 2, an unfinished bibliography, over-asserted prose, and a likely page-limit violation (§9).

### 7. Fit
Yes, this belongs at PALM — retention policy, expiry, deletion, and membership leakage are the workshop's stated topics, and the CFP welcomes theory. I would argue *for* discussion-slot acceptance on the grounds of S1–S3 (the room benefits from a formal counterpoint to bookkeeping-based retention), while stating openly that anyone expecting agent-memory results will find a statistical-physics characterization of a 4-dimensional designed store. I would not fight for it over an empirically grounded competitor.

### 8. The single strongest hostile objection
> "The paper measures, to four significant figures, the retention physics of a memory nobody uses, on a four-dimensional testbed engineered so the theory holds by construction. Its one probe of whether the phenomena survive *learning* answers no: the emergent network has no continuous register (Fig. 8, ≈1.6 bits), the T = 0 latch is admitted to require designed symmetry, and the emergent vault's own pre-registered falsifier fired (App. D). What remains for this community is a beautifully instrumented damped harmonic oscillator and a deletion guarantee that excludes recency eviction — the one policy deployed agent memories actually use."

Does the paper answer it? **Partially, and only in the appendices.** The transfer evidence (V-curve exponents on emergent units, Fig. 7/10; refrigerator law "transfers exactly," Table 4; bias-corrected emergent coset obeying the Goldstone law above T*, Fig. 9) genuinely blunts "nothing survives learning" — but it is scattered across Apps. B–D, while the main text's scope bullets concede the precondition without rebutting the consequence. The "why should this room care absent a task" half of the objection is answered nowhere in the 18 pages; §3 defers it to future work.

**Load-bearing appendix-only content** (a reviewer stopping at p. 4/5 would judge without): the update equation itself (F, Assumptions); the instrument definitions behind the cross-verification claim (C); the 86.97 → 107.77 estimator argument in full (D); the fired falsifier on the emergent contrast arm (D, ℓ375–379 — *this one should be in main text*); the trilemma's operational definitions (E); the entire adversary model behind the 0.983 AUC (E, Table 6).
