87: \begin{abstract}
88: A world model that is to be trusted with requests it has never seen needs three things at
89: once: its outputs must be \emph{valid} under the task's judge, it must \emph{compose}
90: held-out combinations of factors from factors it has seen, and it must be able to
91: \emph{forget} specific training rows. Compositional generation is a crowded area
92: and machine unlearning is an established one. We work at the level of a single \emph{naive unit} and
93: compare it only with other naive units (an MLP, an LSTM, a transformer and a continuous-input MLP):
94: we do not claim the best compositional-generation or unlearning system, and systems built on top of
95: units, such as sharded retraining or compositional diffusion, are out of scope.
96: We study the simplest object that has all three properties, the \emph{naive-CLU} (n-CLU): a
97: label-free set of per-well principal subspaces computed on the latents of a frozen
98: encoder, read by a request-indexed address book and rendered by a frozen decoder, whose
99: content is derived from a retained sufficient statistic. Deleting rows downdates the
100: statistic, not the truncated factors, so the n-CLU after deletion equals the n-CLU fitted
101: without those rows. On a Shapes3D value hold-out, at the finest partition on a validation plateau
102: that the seeds do not resolve, 
103: the n-CLU composes held-out pairs and held-out combinations of every factor, with every landscape
104: control collapsing. 
110: Against read-path-matched baselines fitted on the n-CLU's own rows, each also trained far beyond
111: its selected optimiser steps, the n-CLU produces about seven more distinct
112: valid novel tuples per held-out combination than the strongest, an MLP, and on every rung of an $n$-way ladder
113: it stays ahead of the strongest baseline fitted on the rung's own rows.
116: After a value, a conjunction or a random subset of items is deleted, the n-CLU matches an n-CLU that
117: was never fitted on the deleted rows, and its address book likewise, to floating-point roundoff; the frozen encoder and decoder are outside the
118: object. Deletion has limits, which we print: the book's retained statistic is 
119: as large as its fit data and its refit is slower than retraining a baseline; the deletable object is several times 
120: the earlier design's byte reference in its footprint breakdown; and deleting the conjunction red sphere 
121: changes what the book knows about the shared value: survivors fall by about six tuples per 
122: combination, far more than after a random item deletion.

130: We close with what the naive unit cannot do and with the measured clue toward a
131: dynamical successor: the n-CLU is exactly the zero-dynamics limit of a settling unit, with
132: the zero-step arm bit-identical to the static read.

140: \subsection{Positioning among naive units}
141: \label{sec:positioning}
142: 
143: The naive-CLU (n-CLU) is a \emph{naive AI unit}: one model with no system built around it, in the sense
144: in which an MLP, an LSTM~\citep{hochreiter_long_1997} or a transformer~\citep{vaswani_attention_2023} is a unit. We compare it only with other naive units, an
145: MLP, an LSTM, a transformer and a continuous-input MLP, matched on the read path
146: (Sec.~\ref{sec:object}). We do not claim the best compositional-generation system or the best
147: unlearning system. Systems built on top of units, such as sharded retraining, compositional
148: diffusion and physics-informed variants of an MLP, are out of scope by definition; each is cited
149: in one sentence below, with the unit/system distinction stated.
150: 
151: Generating held-out combinations of factors is an old and active problem~\citep{du_compositional_2020,avidan_compositional_2022}. The multilinear
152: line (\citealp{tenenbaum_separating_2000};
153: TensorFaces~\citep{goos_multilinear_2002}; \citealp{vlasic_face_2005}; MLC-VAE~\citep{georgopoulos_multilinear_2020}) factorises a data tensor organised by every label combination and
154: renders linearly; the frozen-generator line (GANSpace~\citep{harkonen_ganspace_2020}; TCA~\citep{oldfield_tensor_2021}; PandA~\citep{oldfield_panda_2023}, and its
155: 2024 journal version~\citep{oldfield_bilinear_2024}) finds editable directions in a pretrained generator without labels and
156: without an address book. We do not claim either half. In particular we do not claim
157: multilinear or bilinear factored generation, a request-indexed tensor head, tensor-product
158: label conditioning, or the downdate primitive, which follows in one line from the summation
159: form of~\citet{cao_towards_2015} because a covariance is a summation.
160: 
161: Unlearning that matches a retrain without the deleted rows is also established, for $k$-means~\citep{ginart_making_2019}, for bilinear
162: matrix completion~\citep{xu_netflix_2023}, and for ridge heads on frozen backbones~\citep{ceci_exact_2027}, where the most recent work
163: restricts itself explicitly to ridge regression and excludes principal-component,
164: eigendecomposition and generative models. The nearest generative attempts are approximate~\citep{kong_approximate_2022}:
165: feature unlearning for pretrained GANs and VAEs~\citep{moon_feature_2024} unlearns a feature rather than rows and
166: fine-tunes the generator; data redaction from pretrained GANs~\citep{kong_data_2023} states that redaction is a
167: different task from deletion; GAN unlearning by substitution~\citep{sun_generative_2025} is approximate by construction.
168: 
169: A systematic screen of both literatures records what we could find. A forward-citation
170: crawl~\citep{priem_openalex_2022} over every title and abstract citing the canonical works of either side screened
171: about four thousand unique works: those citing the generation-side anchors, those citing the
172: deletion-side anchors, those from orthogonal queries that cite neither, and those from
173: a final targeted sweep. 
174: Deletion language appears in ten of the generation-side works, most of them
175: false positives and none deleting training rows; no generation-side work combines frozen-decoder,
176: tensor-head and label-index language; the highest number of the six claim
177: atoms reached by any single work is four; and of the deletion-side works none combines
178: principal-subspace, generative and frozen-model language. 
180: The orthogonal query that asks the question directly, \emph{compositional} $\wedge$
181: \emph{generative} $\wedge$ a deletion phrase, returns zero works on the screen.
183: These are the screen's counts, not a claim that no such model exists: abstract-level screening
184: cannot see a match buried in a method section, and indexing of preprints younger than about
185: three months is incomplete (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}).
186: Three families of systems built on top of units are out of scope, one sentence each.
187: SISA training~\citep{bourtoule_machine_2021} is a system that makes any unit, a generator included,
188: unlearnable in this sense by splitting its data into shards and retraining the unit on the shard that
189: held the deleted rows, which reads that shard's retained data; we compare with the unit it wraps,
190: whose only route to that match is the retrain of Sec.~\ref{sec:del-comparators}.
191: Compartmentalized diffusion models~\citep{golatkar_training_2024} are a system that
192: trains separate diffusion models on separate data sources, composes them at inference and forgets
193: a source by dropping its model, so what is composed is models of data sources, not held-out
194: combinations of factor values within one unit.
196: Physics-informed variants of an MLP, such as physics-informed neural
197: networks~\citep{raissi_physics-informed_2019} and Hamiltonian neural networks~\citep{greydanus_hamiltonian_2019}, are
198: systems that add a physical loss or structure to an MLP unit, and the baseline we compare with is the
199: plain MLP unit.
201: 
202: One distinction is ours and worth a sentence. The decremental-PCA line
203: (\citealp{hall_adding_2002}, and successors~\citep{jin_evd_2014,lee_fast_2021}) subtracts \emph{deflated} eigenspaces; its
204: authors describe the result as approximating the refit, report that subtraction tends to
205: instability, and give no rigorous error analysis. Our route matches the refit, for the opposite reason:
206: \emph{downdate the statistic, not the truncated factors}. The full, untruncated second-moment
207: statistic is downdated by subtraction and the content is re-eigendecomposed afterwards, so no
208: truncation error ever enters the subtraction.
209: 
210: \subsection{The three properties}
211: \label{sec:why}
212: 
213: A world model~\citep{ha_world_2018,hafner_mastering_2025} is useful to the extent that it can be asked for states and transitions it
214: has not seen~\citep{valevski_diffusion_2025}, and trusted with the answer. We name three properties and use them throughout.
215: \textbf{Rule validity}: an output is valid if the task's judge accepts it (on the maze, the engine's judge, which uses
216: integer arithmetic with no tolerance)~\citep{vafa_evaluating_2024}; approximate similarity to a training item does not count.
217: \textbf{Composition}: the model produces valid outputs for held-out combinations of factor
218: values that it has seen only separately~\citep{zhao_toward_2022}. \textbf{Deletion}: after a set of training rows
219: is deleted, the deletable object matches, to floating-point roundoff, an object that was never fitted
220: on those rows. The three pull against each other: a lookup table deletes in this sense but composes
221: nothing it has not written; a learned generator composes but meets this definition only by retraining
222: (the whole model, or a shard of it~\citep{bourtoule_machine_2021}); and
223: approximate unlearning~\citep{guo_certified_2023,golatkar_eternal_2020} is approximate by construction and does not meet it (Sec.~\ref{sec:del-comparators}).
224: The object we study makes the three properties of one n-CLU, and we measure, row by row, where each
225: holds and where it does not.

227: \subsection{Contributions}
228: \label{sec:contrib}
229: 
230: \begin{enumerate}
231: \item \textbf{Composition at a stated partition, against data-matched, read-path-matched baselines.} On a
232:   Shapes3D~\citep{3dshapes18,kim_disentangling_2019} value hold-out at $\Kstar=32$ wells, three scored seeds, with every 
233:   pre-declared landscape control collapsing, the n-CLU scores above every read-path-matched baseline
234:   fitted on its own rows, with each baseline's convergence printed beside it: by about seven tuples 
235:   per held-out factor combination over the strongest, an MLP, and by more over the transformer 
236:   (Tab.~\ref{tab:t7}, Sec.~\ref{sec:shapes-t7}). On every rung of an $n$-way ladder it 
237:   scores above the strongest baseline fitted on the rung's own rows 
238:   (Tab.~\ref{tab:ladder}, Sec.~\ref{sec:ladder}); for $n\ge3$ the rungs are a training-data axis and not a depth axis.
241: \item \textbf{Deletion on the n-CLU and on its address book, with its limitations printed.} After
242:   a value, conjunction or item deletion, the n-CLU matches an n-CLU that was never fitted on the deleted
243:   rows, and the refit address book matches a book never fitted on them; the match is on the n-CLU and
244:   its address book, and the frozen encoder and decoder are outside the object. We make no general claim
245:   about composition after deletion: deleting a conjunction measurably changes what the book knows 
246:   about the value it shares with the survivors (Sec.~\ref{sec:shapes-del}). The deletion reads no
247:   training row, but the book's retained statistic is nearly a gigabyte, the size of its fit data, and its refit 
248:   takes longer than retraining a baseline (Tabs.~\ref{tab:del} and~\ref{tab:rivals-del}), so this is neither a size nor a speed claim. Refusal after deletion is a 
250:   separate, structural mechanism, a hole in a coupling table (Sec.~\ref{sec:hole}).
251: \item \textbf{The same properties on a maze.} On a colour-maze transition instrument (CMAZE-T) with
252:   symmetric inputs, six seeds, the n-CLU is ahead of
253:   the best matched baseline on three seeds, level with it on two and behind on one
254:   (Tab.~\ref{tab:cmazet-sym}). A colour-relational request coding lifts the n-CLU on every seed, but baselines handed the 
256:   same columns close most of that gap, so it is an input-representation result, not an n-CLU result
257:   (Sec.~\ref{sec:maze-wm}). We offer the transition instrument itself as a minor contribution.
258: \item \textbf{Two ablations and a leaving note.} A composition-only n-CLU composes and cannot
259:   forget; an item memory forgets and cannot compose (Sec.~\ref{sec:ablations}). The naive unit's
260:   limitations, its measured zero-dynamics identity and the theory we carry close the paper
261:   (Secs.~\ref{sec:limits}--\ref{sec:followup}).
262: \end{enumerate}
263: 
264: \noindent\textbf{Thickness, stated in the introduction.} The positive composition claim rests
265: on one environment (Shapes3D) at three scored seeds; no Shapes3D row can exceed three seeds,
266: because seeds 3 and 4 have verified caches but no frozen decoder and no external classifier, so no
267: combination of theirs can be judged. 
268: The maze transition instrument, under the engine's judge, prints six seeds on its words-plus-observation rows
269: (Sec.~\ref{sec:maze-wm}). The rows still pending a run are marked placeholders whose text names the row.

272: \section{Methods}
273: \label{sec:object}
275: 
277: \citet{jawahar2026chlu} introduced the CHLU as a latent dynamics model (defined here as an algorithm
278: that either enforces or extracts the dynamics of the latent space itself to either design or
279: approximately understand semantic rules created by the encoder that produced this abstract space
280: such that downstream tasks can be appropriately dealt with or downstream design choices can be
281: better informed). \citet{jawahar2026chlu} envision their unit (as the name suggests) as a step that
282: provides a clue towards developing better latent dynamics models. We build on top of this idea and
283: drop the complex physics design to construct our naive-CLU as a special case of the CHLU and test it
284: as a factorized memory unit for compositional generation with additional properties such as deletion
285: as a virtue of the design itself.
286: 
287: \paragraph{The n-CLU.} A frozen encoder maps each training item to a latent. The latent
288: space is cut, without labels, into $K$ wells by a balanced correlation partition; in each well
289: the n-CLU keeps the leading principal subspace of the fit pool, with the pool mean as the
290: first atom. No label enters the content. We write $n$ for the number of factors jointly
291: composed by a request and $K$ for the number of wells, and never write both as $k$.
292: 
293: \paragraph{The read.} A request is a tuple of factor values. A tensor-product address book,
294: fitted on the fit pool, maps the request to per-well coefficients; the read renders
295: $x_k=U_k c_k$ in each well's orthonormal basis and the frozen decoder renders the image. The
296: read is request-only: no feature tensor enters it, which is asserted on the deployed object
297: (bitwise stable under feature perturbation over 64 checks at $\Kstar$).
299: Labels therefore touch the model only at the address book. This is the interface split:
300: unsupervised content, a request-indexed head, and a frozen renderer, with no Cartesian-product
301: training requirement~\citep{wiedemer_compositional_2023,lachapelle_additive_2023,montero_lost_2022}.
302: 
303: \paragraph{The memory footprint, never netted.} Every n-CLU row prints four byte counts
304: separately: (1) the atoms, (2) the covariance and count statistic, (3) the address book, and
305: (4) the coupling and hole table used for refusal. We never net them into one number, and every
306: byte figure names its convention (symmetric-triangle float64 for the statistic, unless marked
307: otherwise). The 524,288-byte figure that recurs below is a \emph{reference} column: the n-CLU
308: of the earlier composition-only design was pinned there, and read-path-matched baselines carry
309: about 131,072 float32 parameters at it. 
310: It is not a limit the deletable object meets.
311: 
314: \paragraph{Why a hold-out draw can remove a whole colour relation.} The hold-out removes word
315: combinations from training at random. On some draws, for some colour, it removes every training
316: transition in which the pocket and the target tint share that colour, the combination that decides
317: whether a move is legal. A model that codes colour by identity then has no training evidence for the
318: held-out transitions of that colour and cannot answer them, however it is trained. A model that is
319: also given the relation ``pocket colour equals tint colour'' can: the relation is learned from the
320: other colours, where it is seen, and applies unchanged to the colour whose rows were removed. This is
321: what the relational columns add (Fig.~\ref{fig:maze-wm}(c)), and it is why every seed is reported
322: separately.
324: 
325: \paragraph{The judge and the estimand.} On Shapes3D an external classifier, hash-pinned per
326: seed, judges each rendered image; the metric is the number of \emph{distinct valid novel
327: factor tuples per held-out combination}, a count, with a fixed ceiling for pair and for six-factor
328: combinations (Tab.~\ref{tab:t7}). The estimand is paired over combinations with a margin of 1.0 tuple per held-out combination declared
329: before any number. 
331: Where an experiment declared its success and falsification criteria before its first
332: number, we refer to each as a \emph{declared criterion}, labelled as in that
333: declaration (for example, criterion S1 or criterion S2).
334: Seed count is a column of every table.
335: 
336: \paragraph{The retained statistic and deletion.} Beside the content the n-CLU keeps, per
337: well, the running covariance, mean and count of the fit rows. Deleting rows subtracts their
338: contribution from the statistic, and the atoms are re-eigendecomposed from the downdated
339: statistic. The address book keeps its own retained statistic, per request tuple the count and first
340: moment of its fit rows; deleting rows downdates it, and the book is refit from it. Neither step
341: reads a training row, but the book's statistic is as large as its fit data
342: (Sec.~\ref{sec:shapes-del}). We use one definition of deletion throughout, and state its scope
343: before any table:
344: 
345: \begin{quote}\small
346: \textsc{The match is on the n-CLU and its address book; the frozen encoder and decoder are outside
347: the object.} After deleting a set of rows, the n-CLU matches an n-CLU that was never fitted on the
348: deleted rows, and the address book likewise: content and book equal, to float roundoff, those fitted
349: from the retained rows alone. Neither frozen network is refit at any point. It is
350: not retrain-equivalence of the generative model, it is not a claim that the renderer forgets, and it
351: is not a claim that the answers on the surviving requests are unchanged.
353: \end{quote}
354: 
355: \noindent The frozen encoder and decoder are declared outside the deletable object: they are
356: frozen, so no deletion touches their weights, which may have been fitted with the deleted rows
357: present.
359: The scoping sentence we carry is the one our deletion theory states for its own n-CLU: this is
360: an n-CLU-level guarantee only; the frozen encoder and any residue of past writes in a learned
361: landscape are separate channels, measured separately; we do not claim certified
362: $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ unlearning~\citep{guo_certified_2023}.

405: \subsection{The Shapes3D value hold-out}
406: \label{sec:shapes}
407: 
408: The deletable object is the \emph{single design}: the n-CLU, read and memory footprint of
409: Sec.~\ref{sec:object} on the Shapes3D value hold-out~\citep{montero2021role,schott_visual_2022}, with the partition fixed on validation
410: by a rule frozen before the sweep, $\Kstar=\max\{K : v(5)-\text{mean}_{\text{seeds}}\,v(K)\le
411: 0.02\}$, over $K\in\{5,8,10,12,16,20,24,28,32\}$. Every swept $K$ satisfied the rule, so
412: $\Kstar=32$, the finest partition swept; the sweep range was not extended to manufacture a
413: crossover. 
414: That is the frozen rule's selection; on three seeds, however, the rule cannot separate the plateau
415: $K=5\ldots32$: the seed standard deviation of validation composition exceeds the rule's 0.02
416: tolerance at $K=10$, 16, 20, 24 and 28 (0.0211, 0.0203, 0.0285, 0.0235, 0.0276) and at $\Kstar=32$ 
417: (0.0259), so three seeds do not distinguish $\Kstar$ from its neighbours. 
418: The finding is the plateau itself: composition is flat across $K$ to within its seed spread, while
419: the honest four-part footprint falls to its minimum near $K=24$, 2,094,130 / 2,080,090 / 2,085,810 B 
420: on seeds 0 / 1 / 2, a minimum that is resolvable (seed band 7,060 B against neighbour gaps of 
421: 12,203 B and 30,507 B; App.~\ref{app:kcurve}, Tab.~\ref{tab:kcurve3}). 
422: The main text carries this one $K$. The $K=5$ endpoint of the sweep is reported in
423: App.~\ref{app:k5}, labelled as the endpoint, with its sign stated as a property of $K=5$.

425: \subsection{Baselines and the matching rule}
426: 
427: \paragraph{Baselines: naive against naive.} Learned baselines (MLP, LSTM~\citep{hochreiter_long_1997}, transformer~\citep{vaswani_attention_2023}, and a
428: continuous-input MLP) are matched to the n-CLU's \emph{read path}, and fitted from the same
429: frozen features. At $\Kstar$ the fitted baselines carry the parameter counts of Tab.~\ref{tab:t7}.
432: The memory footprint is not a read-path parameter; it is printed, never matched~\citep{borgeaud_improving_2022,khandelwal_generalization_2020,lample_large_2019}. A lookup table
433: of written items is a printed \emph{floor}, never a baseline. Four controls, which we call the \emph{pre-declared
434: landscape controls} throughout, \emph{landscape removed} (atoms zeroed), \emph{landscape shuffled} (atom values permuted within each well), \emph{shuffled across wells}
435: (well blocks dealt to other wells) and \emph{random atoms} (random atoms in place of the fitted ones), must collapse before any
436: row is claimed for the n-CLU; if they do not, the row is an interface row or nothing.
437: 
438: \paragraph{A second, earlier baseline set.} The ladder (Sec.~\ref{sec:ladder}) also prints an earlier set
439: of read-path-matched baselines, fitted on a smaller combination-split pool, 
440: with one schedule, the same for 
441: every family and seed (Tab.~\ref{tab:ladder}). 
443: The combination-split pool holds out combinations but no factor value, so that set was fitted on
444: rows carrying the held-out \texttt{object\_hue} and shape values that the value hold-out n-CLU never
445: saw; its own record states that such a comparison is not like-for-like and flatters whichever arm
446: saw more, here the baseline. It is printed beside the data-matched set, never in its place.

449: \subsection{The $n$-way ladder protocol}
450: 
451: The ladder asks whether the n-CLU keeps composing as more factors are pinned jointly. A rung
452: $n$ holds out combinations in which $n$ factors are jointly pinned to held-out values; $n=2$ and
453: $n=6$ are the two ends. Tab.~\ref{tab:ladder} gives the n-CLU at $\Kstar$ on every rung and
454: seed beside two read-path-matched baseline sets on the same combinations: the data-matched set, all four
455: families refit on each rung's own pool, and the earlier set, whose features 
456: are a function of the request alone, so that its scores do not depend on the rung's fit pool and on

464: \subsection{The deletion protocol}
465: 
466: Three deletions are run on the $\Kstar$ object on TEST, on its fit pool (Tab.~\ref{tab:del}), at three 
468: seeds. \emph{Value}: every fit row carrying red. \emph{Conjunction}: every fit row carrying red
469: \emph{and} sphere; we call it the red sphere everywhere. The conjunction first named in the design,
470: red $\wedge$ cube, deletes no rows, because cube is the shape held out of the split entirely; it is
471: printed as vacuous and never as a deletion result. \emph{Item}: a random 1\% of fit rows, which is
472: also the control for refit instability. \emph{Forgetting $c$} is three operations on one object:
473: downdate the n-CLU's content; downdate the address book's statistic and refit the book from it; and punch the
474: hole for $c$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:hole}). Every deletion row therefore prints four measurements
475: (Tab.~\ref{tab:del}): (a) the n-CLU's content equals the content refit without the rows, a result; (b) the book
476: equals the book refit without the rows, a result; (c) requests for $c$ are refused through the hole,
477: a display row only, because the hole is a flag and symbolic refusal is not claimed; and (d)
478: composition on the surviving combinations before and after, a result, with the check that the downdated
479: object and the object refit without the rows compose identically.

481: \subsection{The CMAZE-T transition task}
482: \label{sec:maze}
483: 
484: This section asks the n-CLU to \emph{generate} a world model's transitions on a colour maze, under
485: the engine's judge. Baselines are matched on read floats, the landscape controls and the blind columns
486: are printed, and every row prints its seed count (Tabs.~\ref{tab:cmazet} and~\ref{tab:cmazet-sym},
487: App.~\ref{app:mazewm}).
489: 
490: \paragraph{CMAZE-T, a minor contribution: a small world-model task for composition.} We offer the
491: transition instrument to researchers working on composition, as a small task and not as a benchmark.
492: Given a colour-maze state and a move, an arm generates the next state; the answer is four facts
493: (legality, displacement, event, pocket) and the engine's judge scores it with no tolerance
494: (Fig.~\ref{fig:task-cmazet}). Test mazes are generated at random, so a lookup of training mazes
495: cannot transfer, and the cross-maze lookup floors are printed. The request is five words; the
496: held-out families are a never-trained value, unseen two-way word pairs and unseen $n$-way word
497: combinations; the judge accepts or refuses validity; learned blind arms that drop the interaction
498: terms or the colour identity are printed as falsifiers. The task is small, its results differ widely from seed to seed, and it has one engine; those are
499: reasons to report it per seed, not to rank systems on it.

900: \paragraph{Deleting a conjunction changes what the book knows about the shared value.} This is a
901: measured limitation of the unit. Deleting the red sphere lowers composition on the surviving combinations
902: by about six tuples per held-out combination, on all three seeds (Tab.~\ref{tab:del}). At seed 0 the loss is concentrated where

911: not delete from the book at all. A value deletion moves the survivors 
912: by less than the declared one-tuple margin (Tab.~\ref{tab:del}).

915: \paragraph{The probe that bounds the claim.} That the match holds on the n-CLU and its address book,
916: and not beyond, is measurable, not only definitional.
917: The n-CLU has changed: without the address-book carry, the read moves by about 0.6 relative RMS on
918: the requests that ask for red; but it moves as much on the requests that do not ask
919: for red, so the basis change is generic, not targeted. With the carry the read moves
920: only at roundoff. The carry rewrites nearly all of the
921: address book's bytes and touches no data rows.

923: lives in the address book, which the carry rotates and does not refit; this is why the deletion
924: now also downdates the address book's statistics (measurement (b) of Tab.~\ref{tab:del}). The trade-off moved
925: rather than vanished: at $K=5$ the same red deletion was concentrated,
926: with a far lower minimum cosine than a size-matched random deletion of the same number of
927: rows (Tab.~\ref{tab:del}, caption). 
929: The targeting of a deletion is a function of rank/width, and it disappears where the memory footprint
930: is smallest. This is why refusal needs its own mechanism (Sec.~\ref{sec:hole}).

932: \subsection{Deletion against retrained baselines}
933: \label{sec:del-comparators}
934: 
935: A learned baseline's only route to a model never fitted on the rows is to retrain from scratch without them. We ran
936: that route for every data-matched baseline family on the same deleted rows of the same 
937: pool, at the baseline's selected schedule, three seeds (Tabs.~\ref{tab:rivals-del}
938: and~\ref{tab:rivals-del-seeds}, App.~\ref{app:delbase}), which print each retrain's rows read 
939: and time. After the value and item deletions the n-CLU stays ahead of every 
940: retrained baseline on every seed, with every 95\% interval above 
941: zero. After the red-sphere deletion it is ahead on the mean at every seed, but its interval crosses
942: zero against the MLP at seeds 1 and 2 and against the LSTM at seed 2: 
943: the n-CLU's own loss on that deletion, about six tuples per held-out combination, is larger than the 
944: change any retrained baseline shows except the continuous MLP at seed 2, so on 
945: collateral damage the baselines' crude retrain is cleaner than the n-CLU's deletion. Nor is the
946: n-CLU's route faster end to end: its downdate takes seconds, but the book's refit takes longer 
947: than any baseline's retrain (Tabs.~\ref{tab:del} and~\ref{tab:rivals-del}). What the n-CLU's route has that the retrain does not 
949: is the match with a refit without the rows, by construction, and no pool row read. Two caveats travel with these rows: the baselines
950: retrain at their selected schedule, so the MLP (seeds 0 and 1) and continuous-MLP convergence flags
951: of Tab.~\ref{tab:t7} travel with them; and approximate unlearning of the data-matched baselines was not
952: run (App.~\ref{app:notrun}).

957: \subsection{Refusal through the hole}
958: \label{sec:hole}
959: 
960: Refusal is a second mechanism on the same object: a coupling table with \emph{holes}.
961: Punching a hole for a deleted value or conjunction marks the request slot as refused. On the
962: $\Kstar$ object two holes cost a byte each, read no data rows, take no measurable time and refit nothing, in
963: a coupling table whose layout is asserted equal to the field-mode n-CLU's over every pair
964: slot (Tabs.~\ref{tab:del} and~\ref{tab:hole}). 
966: One hole (red $\wedge$ cube) is refusable although no red cube was ever written: refusal does
967: not need a deleted row to have existed. 
968: The hole is the only mechanism on this object that refuses, and it is a flag: a request for a
969: holed value is refused by construction, so refusal is measurement (c) of Tab.~\ref{tab:del}, a
970: display row, and symbolic refusal is not claimed. An item deletion makes nothing impossible: on the n-CLU's own pool, deleting a random
971: 1\% of items clears no value bit and punches no hole, so no request becomes certifiably impossible
972: and the item row has no refusal row at all.

1155: \paragraph{Reading the CMAZE-T panel figures.} Figs.~\ref{fig:cmazet-families}--\ref{fig:cmazet-door-tint}
1156: (App.~\ref{app:mazewm}) draw single held-out transitions with every model's next state. Each panel
1157: is a $5\times5$ crop of the maze centred on the agent's cell at $s_t$. Walls are dark, tinted cells
1158: pale in their colour, doors framed in their colour with a bar across while closed, untaken keys are
1159: diamonds, the goal a cross, and the agent a black disc whose centre shows the colour of the key in its
1160: pocket (white when the pocket is empty). The first column is $s_t$ with the action (an arrow, or a
1161: ring for pick-up), the second the engine's $s_{t+1}$, and each further column the $s_{t+1}$ that a
1162: model's emitted outcome produces. The engine's judge does not score the picture: it scores four
1163: emitted facts, legality, the move, the key event and the pocket after the move, and a picture need
1164: not show all four. So a strip under every $s_{t+1}$ prints the emitted facts (under the engine's
1165: column, the true ones). Its first line reads legal or illegal. Its second line shows the move
1166: ($\uparrow$ $\rightarrow$ $\downarrow$ $\leftarrow$, or a dot for staying), the key event ($+$ and a
1167: colour for a key picked up, $-$ and a colour for a key used at a door, blank for none) and the pocket
1168: after the move (the disc; white is empty). An illegal emission leaves the agent in place and is scored
1169: on its pocket alone, so only the pocket is shown. The tick or cross on a panel is the judge's verdict
1170: on all four facts, so two panels that show the same picture and the same strip never carry opposite
1171: marks.

1178: \subsection{Limitations}
1179: \label{sec:limits-list}
1180: 
1181: \begin{enumerate}
1182: \item \textbf{On the maze transition task the n-CLU's lead is small and seed-dependent.}
1184:   On CMAZE-T with symmetric inputs the n-CLU is ahead of the best matched baseline on three of six seeds,
1185:   level on two and behind on one, and behind on every seed of the full word design 
1186:   (Sec.~\ref{sec:maze-wm}, Tab.~\ref{tab:cmazet-sym}).
1187: \item \textbf{Deleting a conjunction changes what the book knows about the shared value.} After deletion
1188:   the n-CLU and the address book match those never fitted on the deleted rows, but deleting the red
1189:   sphere lowers composition on
1190:   the surviving combinations by 6.13 / 6.43 / 6.30 tuples per held-out combination, 32.1 on the combinations that request red 
1191:   with another shape at seed 0, while a random 1\% deletion moves them by at most 0.11; retraining a 
1192:   baseline without the same rows is cleaner on collateral damage (Sec.~\ref{sec:shapes-del},
1193:   Sec.~\ref{sec:del-comparators}).
1194: \item \textbf{Deletion is not cheap in memory or time: the address book's refit.} The book's retained statistic is 0.97 GB, 
1195:   the size of its fit data, and its refit takes 2,367 to 5,913 s, longer than a baseline's 
1196:   retrain-without; ``reads no training row'' is not a size or a speed claim.
1197: \item \textbf{Deletability requires a large number of bytes.} The deletable object at $\Kstar$ is 4.0768 to 4.0897
1198:   times the reference (Tab.~\ref{tab:ledger-kstar}); across nine swept partitions the honest
1199:   four-part total is U-shaped in $K$, with its minimum 3.9942 times the reference at $K=24$, so
1200:   $\Kstar$ is not the point of smallest footprint; and on the one configuration at $K\ge36$ whose composition
1201:   was measured ($K=40$, 7 atoms per well, 0.991 times the pin) composition collapses to 0.0648.
1202:   No object with a float64 sufficient statistic composes and forgets inside 524,288 bytes at rank
1203:   64 on any swept partition (the validation sweep labels itself a smoke, not a claim).
1205: \item \textbf{The baseline search may be truncated.} Every data-matched family selected the largest
1206:   learning rate and the longer epoch count of its grids, and the MLP at seed 0 still gains 1.47 tuples 
1207:   per held-out combination from ten to thirty times its steps (Sec.~\ref{sec:shapes-t7}).
1208: \item \textbf{The encoder loop licenses no n-CLU claim.} Training the encoder through the n-CLU
1209:   fired the pre-declared falsifier on row 3 of the shortened leg, a falsifier that involves no baseline 
1210:   (App.~\ref{app:encloop}).
1211: \item \textbf{No functioning generative baseline.} LACE and PoE, re-implemented at their own settings
1212:   in this feature space, render no exact-match request on the generation table and score at the level
1213:   of our landscape controls (Tab.~\ref{tab:lace-poe}); the study compares naive units only and has no
1214:   working compositional-generation baseline.
1216: \item \textbf{At most three seeds on Shapes3D.} Seeds 3 and 4 cannot be judged there (no decoder, no
1217:   external classifier), so no Shapes3D row can exceed three seeds; CMAZE-T prints six.
1218: \item \textbf{Further limitations} (an unseen-valid row that belongs to a generic learner, an
1219:   exhausted placement dial, and comparators not run) are listed in App.~\ref{app:morelimits}.
1220: \item \textbf{Residual novelty risk.} Abstract-level screening cannot catch a match buried in a
1221:   method section, and very recent preprints and non-indexed workshop papers are incompletely
1222:   covered; we judge the risk low, not zero. 
1223: \end{enumerate}

1233: \subsection{The zero-dynamics clue}
1234: \label{sec:clue}
1235: 
1236: The n-CLU we evaluate is the zero-dynamics limit of the CLU~\citep{jawahar2026chlu}: the same content, the same wells,
1237: the same read, with the settling loop's step count set to zero. Inside a well this is an
1238: algebraic identity: the n-CLU's energy is the distance to the atom span, the request's basin sits
1239: at an address inside that span, so both minima coincide and an in-well settle has nowhere to go
1240: (derivation in App.~\ref{app:clue}). We asserted this in code: the zero-step arm was required to be bit-identical to
1241: the static read by \texttt{np.array\_equal} over 256 requests, and it was, on all three scored
1242: seeds, returning the static read's held-out scores to every printed digit. 
1244: The only place dynamics can act is across wells: with a bilinear low-rank coupling $\lambda W$
1245: the settled state is $z^\star=(\beta I-\lambda W)^{-1}\beta z_{\text{req}}$, which returns
1246: $z_{\text{req}}$ exactly at $\lambda=0$. Switching the dynamics on lowers composition at every
1247: strength at which they move the state: by less than one six-factor tuple per
1248: held-out combination at the gentlest setting that moves it, and by about forty on the seed mean at full strength, while incurring
1249: bytes of read-side coupling that the static read does not (App.~\ref{app:physics}). 
1251: The identity holds under four named conditions (App.~\ref{app:clue}).
1252: 
1253: \subsection{The Latent Dynamics Model class}
1254: \label{sec:theory}
1255: 
1256: We call the class of objects that this n-CLU is the static limit of a \emph{Latent Dynamics
1257: Model}: a memory whose content is read by relaxing a latent state under a designed energy~\citep{hopfield_neural_1982,krotov_dense_2016,ramsauer_hopfield_2021,hoover_energy_2023,kong_reservoir-computing_2024}, of
1258: which the n-CLU is the member with the relaxation switched off. (The phrase is used in
1259: reduced-order modelling and in model-based reinforcement learning for a learned surrogate of an
1260: environment's dynamics; here the dynamics are the read, not a model of the data.) Its laws are this paper's own theory, each with a status label and rider (further laws in App.~\ref{app:shorts}).
1261: 
1262: \paragraph{Two laws measured on this study's own n-CLU (\textsc{confirmed [this study]}).}
1263: Switched on, the field-mode n-CLU's relaxation obeys a curvature--retention relationship whose
1264: prediction is a \emph{time}, not a score: with nothing fitted, it predicts the settle half-life
1265: well within its declared bar on the held-out combinations and three seeds (Tab.~\ref{tab:crr}), and beats a one-parameter form fitted with sight of the held-out measurements
1266: on every combination on every seed; one of six declared criteria (the V-curve argmin) misses on
1267: all three seeds, and the law predicts where its simplest form stops.
1269: Deletion commutes with scheduled decay on a learned memory, no byte differing in the memory
1270: image, while the stronger claim that the post-delete memory equals the never-inserted one is
1271: \textsc{refuted} (nearly two thirds of the image differs, most of it because the address book
1272: and coupling table are fitted on the pool; App.~\ref{app:laws}). 
1274: \emph{These are dynamics results about our own n-CLU. They occupy no row of the evaluation, they
1275: beat no model, and we make no capability claim from them}; no arm of ours fails the retention law,
1276: so it distinguishes nothing. The full statements, criteria and misses are in
1277: App.~\ref{app:physics}.

1279: \subsection{Summary}
1280: \label{sec:leaving}
1281: 
1282: The naive unit provides deletion by subtraction, matching an n-CLU never fitted on the deleted rows, and, at a stated partition, composition on
1283: Shapes3D that scores above read-path-matched baselines fitted on its own rows, by about seven tuples per
1284: held-out combination over the strongest. On the maze transition task its lead over matched
1285: baselines is small and seed-dependent, and its best configuration is a request coding any learner can be
1286: given; deleting a conjunction removes what the book knew about the shared value; and it incurs the
1287: memory and time cost of that match, in bytes and in the book's refit. Switched on, the dynamics lower composition at
1288: every strength that moves the state, while a law that no arm of ours fails predicts their relaxation:
1289: so far a \emph{law about the n-CLU}, not a \emph{lever on the task}. The experiment that would make
1290: the law a claim about a class, a comparator predicted in advance to be mispredicted in the
1291: $h/h^\star\in[0.5,2]$ bucket, is named and not run.

1347: \section{Reporting conventions}
1348: \label{app:conventions}
1349: 
1350: \paragraph{Provenance.} Nearly every number in the source of this paper carries a comment naming
1351: the record it was read from; the reproducibility statement gives the scope of the verbatim re-check. Numbers are quoted as the record prints them; none is re-derived.
1352: Where two records print the same quantity at different precision, the one cited is the one the
1353: number was taken from.
1354: 
1355: \paragraph{Two byte conventions, and which object carries which.} The statistic of every object
1356: in the main text is counted under the symmetric-triangle float64 convention,
1357: $\sum_k d_k(d_k+1)/2\times8$ B plus means and counts; the $\Kstar$ object's covariance is
1358: 549,120 B on it, which closes to the byte. 
1359: One object is counted under the full-square convention, $8\sum_k d_k^2$: the $K=5$ n-CLU of the
1360: ladder, on the label-derived partition. We name the two $K=5$ objects apart everywhere:
1361: \emph{$K=5$ (MI partition, full square)}, the ladder's n-CLU, well widths 777 / 936 / 216 / 60 /
1362: 59 on seed 0, statistic 12,284,920 B; and \emph{$K=5$ (ward, triangle)}, the crossover and
1363: bridge n-CLU, five wells of width 409--410, statistic 3,391,552 B. They differ in partition and
1364: in convention; the convention alone is a factor of about two.
1366: The ladder's footprint record carries three parts; its coupling table is a declared absence, not a
1367: zero. 
1368: 
1369: \paragraph{Seeds and configurations.} Seed count and configuration count are separate fields. The
1370: phrase ``nine seeds'' never applies: where nine legs appear they are three configurations times
1371: three seeds inside one implementation (App.~\ref{app:impl}).

1947: \section{The zero-dynamics identity: conditions and provenance}
1948: \label{app:clue}
1949: 
1950: \paragraph{The derivation.} The content is an orthonormal basis $U_k$ of the
1951: per-well atom span, and the read renders the request's address through that basis,
1952: $x_k=U_k c_k$. The n-CLU's energy for a basis is the distance to its span,
1953: $E_{\text{content},k}=\tfrac12\operatorname{dist}^2(x_k,\operatorname{span}G_k)$, zero everywhere
1954: on the span; the request enters as a basin at the address, $E_{\text{req},k}=\tfrac12\beta\lVert
1955: x_k-a_k\rVert^2$, zero at the address. Because $a_k\in\operatorname{span}(G_k)$ by construction
1956: of the renderer, both terms attain their minima at the same point, so an in-well settle has
1957: nowhere to go. 
1958: 
1959: \paragraph{The four conditions.} (C1) The n-CLU's energy is distance to the atom span, the atoms
1960: being a basis: with an atoms-as-points energy on the same content the settle moves (relative
1961: displacement median 1.7244, max 2.6682 on a synthetic check). (C2) The address lies in the span:
1962: pushed off the span by $\delta=0.10$ the settle moves by exactly $\delta/(1+\beta)$, measured
1963: 0.050000 against the closed form 0.050000. (C3) The state is carried in span coordinates; the same
1964: energy in channel coordinates drifts by \sci{7.447}{-08} relative, float32 rounding, so the paper
1965: says ``identical'' of the channel form and ``bit-identical'' only of the span form. (C4) No
1966: cross-well term: at $\lambda=0$ the displacement is exactly 0.0; at
1967: $\lambda=10^{-4}$, $10^{-2}$, $0.05$, $0.2$ it is \sci{7.875}{-05}, \sci{7.882}{-03},
1968: \sci{3.971}{-02}, \sci{1.720}{-01}. Two wells of the deployed n-CLU were full rank, where an in-well
1969: settle is trivially a no-op. The coercivity certificate reproduces from first principles:
1970: $\lambda^\star=0.25000000$ and a certificate minimum eigenvalue of $-1.50000000$ at the declared
1971: $\lambda=1$, so the landscape is unbounded below at the declared strength and the used strength is
1972: 0.225000. 
1973: The identity is in-well only; a $\lambda=0$, steps$>0$ arm was never run on the deployed n-CLU (the
1974: synthetic check covers steps 0 / 1 / 10 / 1000); and the \sci{1.9}{-7} off-span residual is the
1975: renderer's float32 rounding, the same size for the static n-CLU that never moves.
1977: 
1978: \paragraph{Provenance of the retention law.} Re-derived from the raw result files, the law's
1979: headline reproduces: 0.043256 / 0.043516 / 0.043102 and $-0.596831$ / $-0.579639$ / $-0.598172$,
1980: 49 of 49 on every seed; the integrator is the theory's map to \sci{1.884682}{-08}; the byte pin is
1981: 524,288 B on every seed; a finite-difference Hessian check reads \sci{3.519263}{-11} /
1982: \sci{1.202332}{-10} / \sci{2.699430}{-11}. 
1983: Its sharpest constant does not: the overdamped slope pair ``$+1.1385$ derived against $+1.1368$
1984: measured'' appears only in prose logs and in no machine-readable record, because the per-cell fit
1985: windows were not saved, and $1+\gamma/(2-\gamma)=1.1385$ sits at $\gamma=0.243303$, not a grid
1986: point. The law's qualitative content (the overdamped slope exceeds $+1$ at every finite $\gamma$)
1987: is verified over the whole band, and $1+\gamma/(2-\gamma)$ is the exact log-derivative (numeric
1988: $+1.138526$ against $+1.138500$). The underdamped slope is confirmed on the theory's own testbeds
1989: ($-1.0020\pm0.0003$) and inconclusive on this n-CLU (measured $-1.1090$ against derived $-1.0050$,

1991: $\sqrt{1-\gamma}$, so a measured floor slope has one degree of freedom, $\gamma$, and one recorded row

2069: \section{Prior derivations}
2070: \label{app:shorts}
2071: 
2072: The laws below
2073: are this paper's own theory. Each carries a status: \textsc{confirmed [this study]} where it was
2074: measured on this study's own n-CLU; \textsc{confirmed [prior derivation]} where it was derived and
2075: validated on designed or small trained testbeds outside this study's n-CLU and was not re-run
2076: here; \textsc{refuted} and \textsc{untested} as stated. Status discipline is part of the section.
2077: 
2078: 
2079: \paragraph{Memory-level deletion in a designed memory (\textsc{confirmed [prior
2080: derivation]}).} With an order-independent waitlist, memory-level deletion leaves a memory
2081: byte-equal to one never written with the deleted items, at every load measured, 0.29 to 1.71 times lattice capacity, a membership signal of AUC 0.5000 $\pm$ 0.0000 on all
2082: six statistics and byte equality 1.0000, under three stated conditions (capacity at least the number
2083: of cells, zero leak, priority- or attribute-based eviction; recency-based eviction is excluded as
2084: intrinsically history-dependent). 
2085: The placement rule is the~\citet{blelloch_strongly_2007} stable-matching table with a global priority order;
2086: the theorems follow from theirs and the fix-up cascade is theirs. Its scoping is the one this
2087: paper adopts in Sec.~\ref{sec:object}: an n-CLU-level guarantee only, the frozen encoder and any
2088: residue of past writes in a learned landscape being separate channels, and no certified
2089: $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ unlearning claimed. 
2090: 
2091: \paragraph{The damping optimum (\textsc{confirmed [prior derivation]}; \textsc{refuted} as a
2092: rollout prediction on this n-CLU).} On the exact one-step Jacobian the emergent retention
2093: half-life is a V-curve in the damping, with its argmin at $0.902\pm0.003$ of
2094: $\gamma_{\text{crit}}$ and slopes $-1.0020$ and $+1.116$ on three of three emergent seeds, one
2095: damping-optimum curve at two values of the curvature rather than two laws.
2097: It holds only with fluctuation--dissipation-consistent noise and a Newtonian kinetic term. On
2098: this study's field-mode n-CLU the rollout misses the same argmin on all three seeds (above), with the
2099: failing step named: the pure-exponential envelope is invalid at the defective point.
2101: 
2102: \paragraph{Interference is degree-bounded (\textsc{confirmed [prior derivation]}).} Cross-unit
2103: basin displacement is exactly zero off the coupling graph (0 of 4656 off-graph entries nonzero
2104: across 72 runs, three topologies), so interference is the degree times the per-edge displacement,
2105: flat in the number of units at fixed degree, while a shared-potential monolith grows as
2106: $N^{1.18\pm0.17}$. The firewall is parameter separation, not block structure.
2108: 
2109: \paragraph{Asserted, not derived (\textsc{untested}).} Eight further statements of the theory,
2110: including a lifetime trilemma whose consequence is measured, have no derivation and no
2111: impossibility argument, and are carried as untested; a certificate family for reach and escape is
2112: untested on this n-CLU, with its own measured limit that unit Jacobian determinant and zero
2113: energy change are not sufficient for bounded dynamics (a certified excursion of 0.09 against
2114: 104.83 blind). 
2115: A law this study planned against, that a Mexican-hat width or separation sets the
2116: interpolation floor and the refusal gap, does not exist in its own derivations, and the nearest
2117: derived object inverts it: the retention floor is curvature-independent.

2119: 
2120: These results were derived and validated on designed or small trained testbeds before this study
2121: and were not re-run on this study's n-CLU; their content enters here as this paper's own theory,
2122: each with the rider that must travel with it. 
2123: \begin{itemize}
2124: \item \textbf{Retention triad (qualitative).} A latch with infinite lifetime, a $\mu^{-2}$ scaling
2125: law and bounded drift are qualitatively absent in learned recurrent baselines (median lifetime 5.6
2126:   / 56 / 69 map-steps for three recurrent families; the emergent unit 263 steps, bounded 0.35 rad).
2127:   Rider: a per-step compute factor of 6.2 against an LSTM inverts retention per unit of compute, so
2128:   no ``longer'' claim is made on compute. 
2129: \item \textbf{GMOR~\citep{gell-mann_behavior_1968} on trained checkpoints.} With a linear ambient spurion the three objects satisfy
2130:   $\mu^2F^2=\delta\Sigma$ to a maximum absolute deviation of \sci{1.33}{-15} on 8 trained SO(2)
2131:   checkpoints. Rider: designed SO(2), probe-only, float64. 
2132: \item \textbf{Erosion law and anchor cure.} Contrastive wake--sleep training inverts a designed
2133:   vacuum if and only if the flat direction is unconstrained by the wake phase; an anchor cures it,
2134:   and every headline law survives past the erosion horizon under the cure. 
2135: \item \textbf{Friction preserves, temperature erases.} The coset diffusion law is verified to
2136:   $1.0068\pm0.0219$ over 25 cells; damping 0.05 to 0.2 lengthens coset half-life
2137:   $3.77\pm0.23$ times. Rider: two observables, two signs; a figure that does not name the initial
2138:   condition is meaningless. 
2139: \item \textbf{The friction-hole vault.} $107.77\pm4.78$ times (the $\hat D$-estimator number; raw
2140:   first-passage $86.97\pm2.94$), against a scalar friction of the same strength at
2141:   $13.28\pm0.12$. 
2142: \item \textbf{Dissipative channel.} Sync time within 7.5\% of a registered prediction; the half-life
2143:   claim is ranking-only; the retention law holds at $N\in\{2,4,8,16\}$. 
2144: \item \textbf{Reversible O(1)-memory training.} At zero damping, recompute-backwards training
2145:   matches saved-activation gradients to \sci{2.1}{-6} relative in float32, with peak activation
2146:   memory 6.3 KB against 6.00 MB at $T=1024$; exact only at zero damping. 
2147: \item \textbf{Hard read isolation (designed memory only).} A sub-barrier particle cannot read a
2148:   foreign item; the predicted threshold mass 0.36 is bracketed by the sweep (crosses at 0.34,
2149:   confines at 0.38). Rider: it does not transfer to a trained block. 
2150: \item \textbf{The trilemma.} With a quadratic payload channel, an anti-decoration guard and a fixed
2151: read size, a memory cannot have all three of exact value fidelity, amplitude-independent address
2152:   hold and amplitude-independent read latency; both proposed fixes are refuted.
2154: \end{itemize}

2156: depends on the encoder, measured separately.

2415: \subsection*{Light encoder adaptation (secondary)}
2416: 
2417: The frozen encoder is the primary object: a harness on top of a large encoder that is never
2418: unfrozen. Light adaptation is optional, and this is what it yields. At $\Kstar$, three seeds,
2419: equal steps and images across arms (400 steps, 25,600 images; encoder parameters in the
2420: optimiser 0 / 131,072 / 428,576), the margin of the single design over the best learned
2421: baseline grows from the frozen encoder under the \emph{anchor} arm (full unfreeze with a
2422: penalty on distance from the frozen weights) on all three seeds: $+0.5688\to+1.3303$,
2423: $-0.0275\to-0.0092$, $+1.1009\to+1.3028$. The low-rank \emph{adapter} grows it on two of
2424: three ($+1.6239$, $+0.3670$, and $+0.3028$, a loss of $0.7982$ on seed 2).
2426: The comparator here is a 180k-parameter MLP, a different opponent from Tab.~\ref{tab:t7}, so
2427: the two tables are never merged; a factor probe measured as a margin over random
2428: initialisation changes nothing ($+0.0001$ to $-0.0160$ aggregate, per seed, never pooled).

2431: \subsection*{Unfreezing with the n-CLU on the gradient path: the decomposition}
2432: 
2433: An earlier CPU leg (shortened decoder, 400 joint steps, 8,192 training images, 3 seeds; a
2434: different instrument, reported in its own table, App.~\ref{app:encloop}) asked whether
2435: training the encoder through the n-CLU improves composition. The net is a wash because two
2436: real effects cancel: unfreezing the encoder with the n-CLU on the gradient path hurts ($A'$
2437: negative on 5 of 6 seed-rows) and a swap loss repairs that damage ($A''$ positive on 4 of 6;
2438: the four two-sided sign-test $p$-values are 0.0001751, 0.000198, 0.000346 and 0.0001564, three
2439: of them at or below 0.0003). This is a finding about the encoder, and the claim
2440: that the n-CLU shapes the representation is not made. 
2441: A longer leg at ten times the steps (4,000 joint steps, 256,000 images per arm) corroborates
2442: the direction of the swap-loss ingredient on its probe margin, $A''$ positive on all three
2443: seeds ($+0.0100$ / $+0.0129$ / $+0.0075$), while its equal-seconds control is short by 41.2 /
2444: 27.8 / 18.6 s and is biased toward the treatment. 
2445: \paragraph{The decomposition re-stated on the longer leg.} On the
2446: longer, full-decoder leg (Tab.~\ref{tab:bard-gpu}, a block of its own, never merged with the
2447: shortened-decoder leg of Tab.~\ref{tab:encloop}) the ordering is \textbf{not reproduced} by its
2448: pre-declared rule, which asks for $A'<0$ and $A''>0$ each in at least four of six cells: $A'$ is
2449: negative in only 2 of 6 cells, while $A''$ is positive in 6 of 6 (its interval excludes 0 in 5 of 
2450: 6). The swap-loss half survives and strengthens (positive in 4 of 6 cells on the shortened leg, 6 
2451: of 6 on the longer one); the ``unfreezing hurts'' half does not survive. Unfreezing \emph{helps} by
2452: about +32 tuples per held-out combination on row 4 at seeds 0 and 2 and hurts only at seed 1, the one seed whose 
2453: \texttt{alone} control trained well (probe margin over random initialisation $-0.0505$ / 
2454: $+0.0242$ / $-0.0862$; row-4 score 38.5 / 64.4 / 39.3). ``Unfreezing hurts'' is therefore not a 
2455: general finding: at these pins it is a property of one seed's well-trained control. What both legs
2456: support is that the swap loss helps.

2458: \section{The two laws on this study's n-CLU, stated in full}
2459: \label{app:laws}
2460: 
2461: \paragraph{Retention on the n-CLU (\textsc{confirmed [this study]}).} Switched on, the field-mode
2462: n-CLU's relaxation obeys a curvature--retention relationship (CRR) whose prediction is a
2463: \emph{time}, not a score. On the damped velocity-Verlet map the n-CLU deploys, the slow
2464: multiplier gives $n_{1/2}=\ln2/(-\ln\lvert\lambda_{\text{slow}}\rvert)$ with nothing fitted. On 49
2465: held-out combinations and three seeds the median $\lvert\log_{10}(n_{\text{meas}}/n_{\text{pred}})\rvert$ is
2466: 0.0433 / 0.0435 / 0.0431 against a declared bar of 0.10, a factor of 1.105 at the median
2467: combination, seed spread 0.0004; and it beats a one-parameter functional form \emph{fitted with sight
2468: of the held-out measurements} on 49 of 49 combinations on every seed (mean paired $\Delta$ $-0.5968$ /
2469: $-0.5796$ / $-0.5982$; that fitted form's own median error is 0.640132 / 0.623155 / 0.641281).
2471: On an exact quadratic, where the law holds by construction, the same measurement chain recovers it
2472: only to a median of 0.0349 (p90 0.0749, max 0.1601 over 17 cells), so the 0.10 bar is about three
2473: times the instrument's floor. 
2474: The law predicts where its own simplest form stops: the median error is 0.0008 at $h/h^\star<0.1$
2475: and 0.1482 in the one bucket straddling the exceptional point, where the $2\times2$ map is
2476: defective. 
2477: One of the six declared criteria missed on all three seeds: the V-curve argmin sits at 0.3362 /
2478: 0.3301 / 0.3520 of $\gamma_{\text{crit}}$ against a factor-2 bar, a miss declared with its
2479: cause before the run; and the stricter reading of a second bar, its maximum over $\gamma$, is
2480: printed and not moved: 59.1158 / 0.1612 / 1883.2934 against 0.10.
2482: \emph{This is a dynamics result about our own n-CLU. It occupies no row of the evaluation, it
2483: beats no model, and we make no capability claim from it}; no arm of ours fails the law, so the law
2484: distinguishes nothing, which is why it is theory and not a result row.
2485: 
2486: \paragraph{Deletion commutes with decay (\textsc{confirmed [this study]}) and its companion
2487: (\textsc{refuted}).} On a learned memory with scheduled per-item decay,
2488: $\text{evict}\circ\text{decay}^n=\text{decay}^n\circ\text{evict}$ with 0 bytes differing in a
2489: 2,054,866-byte image, 12 of 12 cells per seed, three seeds, zero tolerance.
2491: Its companion, that the post-delete memory is byte-identical to the memory built from the
2492: never-inserted history, fails as declared in advance: 1,339,083 / 1,338,974 / 1,343,641 bytes differ
2493: (65.17\% / 65.16\% / 65.39\% of the image), and the break-out shows that placement is only 16.5\%
2494: of the obstruction and 83.5\% is that the address book and coupling table are fitted on the pool.
2496: That falsifies the precondition (canonical Blelloch--Golovin placement) on this memory, not the
2497: law. Two consequences derived before measurement were then exact: the refusal horizon is $19.9316$
2498: half-lives for every curvature, measured at 539 steps $=19.9432$ half-lives on all three seeds.
